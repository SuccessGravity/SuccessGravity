Best Shopify Alternatives in 2026 (Compared)

Six honest alternatives to Shopify — compared on price, transaction fees, ease of use, and who each platform genuinely serves best.

Last updated: October 2026 · 6 alternatives compared · Independently reviewed

By David · Founder & Editor, Success Gravity · October 2026

What Is Shopify — and Why Are Businesses Looking for Alternatives?

Shopify is the dominant hosted e-commerce platform, powering millions of online stores worldwide. Its appeal is clear: a polished admin interface, a vast app ecosystem, reliable hosting, and multi-channel selling (web, social, point-of-sale) all under one roof. For anyone launching a product-based business without technical resources, Shopify provides a faster path to a professional-looking store than almost any other option.

That said, a growing number of merchants look for alternatives as their stores mature — or before they even sign up. The core complaints are cost and complexity. Shopify's Basic plan starts at $29/month billed monthly ($25/month on annual), but the real cost is substantially higher once you factor in the apps most stores need: email marketing, loyalty programs, reviews, upsells, SEO tools. A realistic mid-tier Shopify store can run $100–$300/month in platform and app fees combined. On top of that, Shopify charges transaction fees (0.5%–2%, depending on your plan) unless you use Shopify Payments — which is not available in every country. For merchants processing high volume or operating in markets where Shopify Payments isn't an option, those fees add up fast. Smaller stores also find Shopify's feature set — designed for scalability — more complex than they need, and the learning curve steeper than expected.

The 6 Best Shopify Alternatives in 2026

These platforms cover every budget and use case, from free WordPress plugins to polished all-in-one builders. We've noted which type of merchant each one suits better than Shopify.

1. WooCommerce

Best for WordPress users

WooCommerce is a free, open-source e-commerce plugin for WordPress that powers roughly 38% of all online stores globally (according to WooCommerce's own market data). If your business already has a WordPress site — or you prefer the content flexibility WordPress gives you — WooCommerce is the most natural path to adding a store. Unlike Shopify, you host and control everything yourself, which means lower platform costs but more responsibility for performance, security, and updates.

The plugin itself is free, so your primary ongoing cost is hosting. Shared WordPress hosting starts at $5–10/month from budget providers; managed WordPress hosting from providers focused on performance runs $20–50/month for most small stores. WooCommerce extensions (the equivalent of Shopify's apps) are available in free and paid tiers — popular ones cover subscriptions, bookings, and advanced shipping. Most essential features (variable products, coupons, tax calculations, basic shipping) are included at no extra cost, which is a meaningful difference from Shopify's app-dependent model.

WooCommerce suits merchants who already live in WordPress, want full ownership of their data and codebase, or need highly customized store logic that Shopify's templates don't accommodate. It is not ideal for merchants who want infrastructure handled for them — server management, security patches, and performance tuning fall on you or your developer.

Starting price: Free plugin + hosting from ~$5–20/month.
Transaction fees: None from WooCommerce itself — you pay your payment processor's standard rate only.
Best suited for: Existing WordPress users, content-heavy stores, developers who want full control.

+ Free plugin — no platform fee ·+ Full data ownership and code access ·+ No transaction fees beyond processor ·− Requires self-managed hosting ·− More technical setup than Shopify

2. Wix

Best for small stores and service businesses

Wix is a drag-and-drop website builder with built-in e-commerce capabilities on its Business plans. The $17/month Business plan (billed annually) includes an online store, payment processing, and marketing tools — making it one of the more affordable all-in-one options for merchants selling fewer than a few hundred products. Wix's editor is genuinely more intuitive than Shopify's for non-technical users, and the template quality has improved significantly in recent years.

Where Wix differs from Shopify most visibly is in its flexibility for blended businesses — stores that sell physical products alongside services, bookings, events, or memberships. Shopify is optimized almost entirely for product-based retail; Wix handles mixed business models more naturally and without additional apps. For a yoga studio that also sells merchandise, or a consultant who also sells digital downloads, Wix's broader feature set is a genuine advantage.

Wix's limitations become apparent at scale: inventory management, complex shipping rules, and multi-channel selling (selling on Amazon, Instagram, and in person simultaneously) are areas where Shopify has a deeper feature set. For merchants expecting to grow past a few hundred orders a month, the platform ceiling is lower than Shopify's.

Starting price: Business plan from $17/month (billed annually).
Transaction fees: None from Wix — payment processor fees only.
Best suited for: Small stores, service-product hybrid businesses, first-time store builders.

+ Very intuitive drag-and-drop editor ·+ Lower entry cost than Shopify ·+ Good for blended service/product businesses ·− Lower ceiling for high-volume stores ·− Less robust multi-channel selling

3. BigCommerce

Best for scaling stores, no transaction fees

BigCommerce is the most direct competitor to Shopify for merchants who are serious about growth but frustrated by Shopify's transaction fee structure. BigCommerce charges zero transaction fees on all plans — you pay only your payment processor's rate, regardless of which processor you use. For merchants processing significant volume through PayPal, Stripe, or a region-specific gateway, this difference alone can justify switching.

BigCommerce's Standard plan starts at $29/month (the same as Shopify Basic) and includes features that require paid apps in Shopify: customer groups, faceted search (product filtering by multiple attributes), real-time carrier shipping rates, and product reviews. Its SEO architecture is also generally considered stronger out of the box — fully customizable URL structures, automatic microdata markup, and built-in redirects — which matters for stores relying on organic search traffic.

The trade-off is ecosystem size. Shopify's app store has more integrations and a larger developer community. BigCommerce's theme selection is smaller, and some third-party tools still prioritize Shopify first. For merchants comfortable with a slightly smaller ecosystem in exchange for no transaction fees and stronger native features, BigCommerce is the most compelling Shopify alternative in this group.

Starting price: Standard plan from $29/month (billed monthly).
Transaction fees: None — payment processor fees only.
Best suited for: Growing stores wanting no transaction fees, SEO-focused merchants, B2B sellers.

+ Zero transaction fees on all plans ·+ Stronger built-in SEO than Shopify ·+ More native features vs Shopify's app model ·− Smaller app ecosystem than Shopify ·− Fewer themes to choose from
Visit BigCommerce

4. Squarespace

Best for design-focused stores

Squarespace has long been the choice for businesses where visual presentation is as important as the store itself — creative agencies, photographers, independent fashion brands, wellness businesses, and anyone whose product images are a core part of the sell. Its templates are widely regarded as the most design-polished of any all-in-one builder, and the Commerce Basic plan at $26/month (billed annually) includes e-commerce features, no transaction fees, and a clean, unified content management experience for both the storefront and editorial content.

Squarespace's approach keeps everything in a single coherent environment: your blog, portfolio, online store, and about pages all share the same design system and are managed from the same dashboard. For Shopify users who find themselves frustrated by the disconnection between Shopify's store pages and any blog or content pages (which require separate apps or workarounds), Squarespace's integrated approach is a meaningful quality-of-life improvement.

Squarespace is less suitable for stores with large product catalogs, complex inventory rules, or high transaction volumes. Its app ecosystem is intentionally minimal compared to Shopify's, which means customization options are more limited. For stores that prioritize aesthetics over catalog complexity, it competes well at a lower price point than Shopify.

Starting price: Commerce Basic from $26/month (billed annually).
Transaction fees: None on Commerce plans — payment processor fees only.
Best suited for: Creative businesses, small-to-medium product catalogs, brand-led storefronts.

+ Industry-leading design templates ·+ Unified store + content management ·+ No transaction fees on Commerce plans ·− Small app ecosystem ·− Not ideal for large or complex catalogs

5. Hostinger Website Builder

Best budget pick

Hostinger's Website Builder is the most affordable complete e-commerce solution in this comparison. Hostinger's pricing page lists e-commerce-capable plans starting from around $2.99/month (billed on a multi-year plan), making it the only option here that genuinely costs a fraction of Shopify's price — not just modestly cheaper. The builder includes an AI-assisted site creation tool, a drag-and-drop editor, product listings, payment integrations, and a custom domain.

The platform is designed primarily for small stores and first-time entrepreneurs who want to be up and running quickly without technical knowledge and without paying Shopify-level fees while they test their concept. Feature depth is narrower than Shopify, BigCommerce, or even WooCommerce at scale — there is no equivalent to Shopify's extensive app marketplace — but for merchants selling a limited range of products to a defined audience, the included feature set is often sufficient.

Hostinger also offers managed WordPress hosting if you later want to migrate to WooCommerce, making it a reasonable starting point for businesses that anticipate scaling: you can run a simple builder store cheaply while validating demand, then move to a more powerful platform without switching hosting providers. For tight-budget launches and lean MVPs, Hostinger's pricing is difficult to match among hosted e-commerce solutions.

Starting price: From ~$2.99/month (billed multi-year).
Transaction fees: None from Hostinger — payment processor fees only.
Best suited for: Budget-conscious first-time store owners, MVP launches, micro-businesses.

+ Lowest price of any option in this list ·+ AI-assisted site builder ·+ Easy upgrade path to WordPress/WooCommerce ·− Narrower features than Shopify at scale ·− Smaller app/extension ecosystem
Visit Hostinger

6. GoDaddy Online Store

Best for simplicity + domain in one

GoDaddy's online store builder is the quickest setup option in this comparison, designed for business owners who want to get a basic store live in an afternoon with minimal configuration. GoDaddy's e-commerce Commerce plan is listed at $9.99/month (billed annually on their pricing page as of this writing), which makes it more affordable than Shopify's Basic plan while including a custom domain, SSL, and payment processing in a single package.

GoDaddy's strength is its consolidation: if you already use GoDaddy for your domain and email, adding an online store from the same dashboard removes the friction of managing multiple providers. The store builder is simpler than Shopify's — by design — with guided setup, AI-generated product descriptions, and fewer customization options. That simplicity cuts both ways: onboarding is faster, but the ceiling on what you can build is lower.

GoDaddy is most appropriate for local businesses (restaurants, service providers, salons) adding a limited product or booking capability to an existing website presence, rather than merchants building a standalone e-commerce business. For straightforward, low-volume selling from a business that primarily operates offline, it competes strongly on price and ease of use. Serious e-commerce stores looking to scale will outgrow it faster than the other options in this list.

Starting price: Commerce plan from $9.99/month (billed annually).
Transaction fees: None from GoDaddy — payment processor fees only.
Best suited for: Local businesses, simple product catalogs, existing GoDaddy customers.

+ Very fast setup ·+ Affordable all-in-one pricing ·+ Domain, email, and store in one account ·− Limited customization and scalability ·− Less feature-rich than Shopify or BigCommerce
Visit GoDaddy

When Shopify Still Makes Sense

Despite the strong alternatives above, Shopify remains the best choice for a specific type of merchant. If you plan to sell across multiple channels simultaneously — your own storefront, Instagram Shopping, TikTok Shop, Amazon, and in person via POS — Shopify's multi-channel infrastructure is ahead of any option in this list. Its app marketplace has more than 8,000 integrations, and the Shopify Payments gateway (where available) eliminates transaction fees while offering a clean, integrated checkout experience. For brands that expect to scale quickly and will benefit from Shopify's network of third-party developers and agencies, the platform premium is justified.

The key question is whether you're paying for the platform or for the ecosystem. If you need the ecosystem — the integrations, the POS hardware, the built-in fraud tools, the massive developer community — Shopify earns its cost. If you're building a focused store that primarily sells through your own website and doesn't need enterprise-grade multi-channel selling, any of the alternatives above will deliver a lower total cost of ownership with fewer compromises than you might expect.

How to Choose the Right Shopify Alternative

  • Already on WordPress? WooCommerce is the obvious choice — zero additional platform cost and full integration with your existing site.
  • Design is a priority? Squarespace's template quality is unmatched in this group, and its unified content/store management is cleaner than Shopify's.
  • Transaction fees are costing you? BigCommerce's zero-fee policy on all plans is the clearest financial argument in this comparison.
  • Budget is the top constraint? Hostinger's pricing is in a different league from the rest — ideal for validating a concept before committing to a more expensive platform.
  • Want everything in one place? GoDaddy bundles domain, email, and storefront at the lowest combined price of any hosted provider here.
  • Building a mixed service and product business? Wix handles booking, events, services, and products better than any platform in this list — including Shopify.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cheapest alternative to Shopify?

Hostinger Website Builder is the most affordable option, with e-commerce plans starting from around $2.99/month (billed multi-year). WooCommerce is technically free as a plugin, though you still need web hosting — budget shared hosting can bring total costs to $5–10/month for a small store. Both are significantly cheaper than Shopify's $29/month Basic plan.

Which Shopify alternative has no transaction fees?

BigCommerce charges no transaction fees on any of its plans — you pay only your payment processor's standard rate. Squarespace also has no transaction fees on its Commerce plans. Wix, GoDaddy, and Hostinger similarly don't add platform-level transaction fees. Shopify waives its own transaction fee only when you use Shopify Payments, which is not available in every country.

Is WooCommerce better than Shopify for WordPress users?

For businesses already running a WordPress site, WooCommerce is typically the better choice. It integrates directly with an existing WordPress installation, keeps all content on the same platform, and avoids paying for a separate Shopify subscription on top of hosting. WooCommerce also offers greater customization through thousands of plugins and themes. The trade-off is that it requires more technical management — hosting, updates, and security fall on you rather than the platform.

Which e-commerce platform is easiest for beginners?

Wix and GoDaddy Online Store are generally the easiest starting points for beginners, with drag-and-drop editors, guided setup flows, and no coding required. Squarespace requires slightly more effort but produces more professional-looking results out of the box. Hostinger's AI-assisted builder also makes initial setup fast for those prioritizing low cost. Shopify is also beginner-friendly, but its strength is in growing stores where app integrations and multi-channel selling justify the complexity.
