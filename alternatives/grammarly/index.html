Best Grammarly Alternatives in 2026 (Compared)

Seven writing tools compared on price, grammar depth, AI features, and who each actually serves best — from free checkers to full AI writing assistants.

By David · Founder & Editor, Success Gravity · Last updated: October 2026 · 7 alternatives compared

What Is Grammarly?

Grammarly is the dominant writing assistant in the consumer and professional markets, with over 30 million daily active users according to the company's own public statements. Its browser extension and desktop app catch grammar errors, flag passive voice, suggest vocabulary upgrades, and — on paid plans — analyze tone and generate full sentences with its AI features. The combination of real-time inline corrections and deeper writing analysis in the sidebar has made it the default tool for anyone who writes English professionally.

Grammarly's free tier handles basic grammar and spelling. Its Premium plan adds style suggestions, clarity rewrites, and tone detection. The Business tier layers in brand tone guidelines, a team style guide, and analytics across an organization's writing — a meaningful upgrade for larger teams.

Why Look for a Grammarly Alternative?

The most common reasons users look elsewhere center on three issues: price, scope, and feature overlap. Grammarly Premium costs around $12 per month on an annual plan — reasonable for heavy users, but steep if you only need an occasional grammar check. Several free tools, notably LanguageTool and Hemingway Editor's web version, handle the most common writing corrections without a subscription.

Scope matters too. Grammarly is built around correcting your existing writing; it doesn't generate content from scratch. If your goal is AI-assisted drafting — writing first drafts, rephrasing whole paragraphs, or producing marketing copy at scale — tools like Jasper AI or QuillBot's paraphrasing engine are purpose-built for those tasks in ways Grammarly isn't. Conversely, writers who produce long-form content (books, screenplays, research papers) often find ProWritingAid's deep structural analysis more valuable than Grammarly's sentence-level corrections. Knowing what you actually need is the fastest way to pick the right alternative.

The 7 Best Grammarly Alternatives

Each option below addresses a specific gap in what Grammarly offers — whether that's price, depth of analysis, AI generation, or multilingual support.

1. ProWritingAid

Best for long-form writing
★★★★★ 4.7/5

ProWritingAid is the most thorough editing tool for writers who produce long-form content — novels, reports, academic papers, long articles. Where Grammarly checks one sentence at a time, ProWritingAid runs 20+ in-depth writing reports on your entire document: overused words, sentence length variation, repeated phrases, readability by paragraph, pacing, dialogue tags, and more. Its Scrivener and Word integrations make it the clear choice for fiction writers who want a professional editorial pass before going to a human editor.

The tool also covers standard grammar and style checking, so it's not purely a structural analysis tool. On short-form writing, however, it generates more suggestions than most users want to process — it's built for the kind of writer who reads every note.

Key strength: 20+ writing reports covering structure, pacing, and style beyond what grammar checkers address.
Best for: Fiction writers, academics, copywriters, and anyone producing 1,000+ word pieces regularly.
Starting price: Free limited plan / Premium from ~$10/month (annual plans available)

+ Deepest writing analysis · + Scrivener + Word integration · − Information overload for short pieces · − Interface less polished
Try ProWritingAid

2. Hemingway Editor

Best for clarity and readability
★★★★★ 4.3/5

Hemingway Editor solves one problem better than any other tool: it forces you to write clearly. The color-coded highlights show sentences that are too long, adverbs that weaken your prose, passive voice, and phrases that have simpler alternatives. The Hemingway Grade gives your writing a reading-level score — the lower, the more accessible. It doesn't try to replace a grammar checker; it's a readability auditor that makes Grammarly-clean writing actually readable at a glance.

The web version at hemingwayapp.com is completely free with no sign-in required — paste your text, read the highlights, and walk away. The desktop app ($19.99 one-time purchase per Hemingway's site) adds offline access and direct publishing to WordPress and Medium. There is no subscription, no AI generation, and no grammar correction beyond what's in the readability pass — that focused scope is part of what makes it uniquely useful.

Key strength: The clearest, fastest readability audit available — free, no account needed.
Best for: Content marketers, journalists, and anyone who wants plain-language writing without complexity.
Starting price: Free (web) / $19.99 one-time (desktop, per Hemingway's pricing page)

+ Free web version · + No subscription needed · − No grammar correction · − No browser extension
Try Hemingway Editor

3. LanguageTool

Best free multilingual checker
★★★★★ 4.4/5

LanguageTool is the strongest free grammar checker for anyone who writes in a language other than English — it supports over 25 languages including German, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Dutch, and more, with native-level grammar rule sets for each. That alone puts it in a different category from Grammarly, which is primarily an English tool. LanguageTool's free plan covers spelling and grammar checks via browser extension; the Premium tier adds advanced style rules, a personal dictionary, and a higher character limit per check.

For English-only writers, LanguageTool covers the essentials well but doesn't match Grammarly's tone analysis or AI rewriting suggestions. It is, however, open-source at its core, and a self-hosted version exists for privacy-conscious organizations — a meaningful distinction for anyone handling sensitive documents. LanguageTool's pricing page lists Premium at significantly less per month than Grammarly on annual billing.

Key strength: Free multilingual grammar checking across 25+ languages; open-source core for privacy-conscious teams.
Best for: Non-English writers, multilingual teams, academics, and users who want a capable free tier.
Starting price: Free / Premium from ~$5/month (annual billing, verify on languagetool.org)

+ 25+ languages · + Strong free tier · − Lighter AI features · − Less tone analysis than Grammarly
Try LanguageTool

4. QuillBot

Best for paraphrasing
★★★★★ 4.4/5

QuillBot's core product is a paraphrasing engine, and it's the best one available. Paste a sentence or paragraph, select a mode (Standard, Formal, Academic, Simple, Creative, Expand, or Shorten), and QuillBot rewrites the text while preserving its meaning. This is genuinely useful for students restructuring source material, marketers adapting copy for different audiences, and ESL writers who want to produce more natural-sounding sentences. The Grammar Checker feature catches basic errors, but it's secondary to the paraphraser.

QuillBot's free tier allows paraphrasing up to 125 words at a time and includes two writing modes; Premium removes the word limit and unlocks all seven modes. The pricing page shows monthly plans around $10 or annual plans at a steep discount — check quillbot.com for current rates. A Chrome extension makes it accessible directly in Google Docs and other web editors.

Key strength: Best-in-class AI paraphrasing in multiple styles — a tool Grammarly doesn't compete with.
Best for: Students, ESL writers, marketers adapting content across formats, and researchers rewording sources.
Starting price: Free (limited) / Premium from ~$9.95/month (verify on quillbot.com)

+ Best paraphrasing tool · + Free tier available · − Grammar check is basic · − Not a full writing assistant
Try QuillBot

5. Wordtune

Best AI rewriting in context
★★★★★ 4.3/5

Wordtune sits between a grammar checker and a full AI writing assistant. Its core feature is AI-powered sentence rewriting: highlight a sentence, and Wordtune suggests multiple rewrites in different tones (casual, formal), lengths (shorten, expand), or styles. Unlike QuillBot, which processes chunks of text in bulk, Wordtune is designed to work sentence-by-sentence within a document, making suggestions feel like inline editorial comments rather than a separate paraphrasing tool.

Higher-tier plans add a Smart Suggestions mode that proactively surfaces rewrites without being asked, and a Spices feature that suggests examples, statistics, counterarguments, and transitions based on what you're writing. Wordtune's free plan is limited but functional; paid plans are in the $10–$15/month range on annual billing — check wordtune.com for current pricing. It integrates with Google Docs, Gmail, and via a Chrome extension across most writing environments.

Key strength: Inline contextual rewrites with tone and length control — a different workflow than Grammarly's correction model.
Best for: Business writers, email professionals, and content teams who want faster, polished drafts without leaving their document.
Starting price: Free limited / Plus from ~$9.99/month (verify on wordtune.com)

+ AI rewrites in context · + Google Docs integration · − Not a grammar checker · − Free tier is limited
Try Wordtune

6. Copy.ai

Best for marketing copy generation
★★★★★ 4.3/5

Copy.ai is an AI writing tool built for marketing and sales copy rather than grammar correction. Where Grammarly starts from your existing draft and suggests improvements, Copy.ai starts from a blank prompt and generates first drafts of ad copy, product descriptions, landing page headlines, email sequences, social captions, and blog outlines. Its template library covers over 90 copy formats, making it faster to start a new piece than to open a blank document and write from scratch.

Copy.ai added a workflow automation layer in 2024 that lets teams chain AI tasks together — research, draft, refine, export — without manual intervention at each step. This makes it a meaningful alternative not just to Grammarly but to a broader content production workflow. The free tier offers limited monthly word generation; the Pro plan at ~$49/month removes limits and adds workflow features. Check copy.ai for current pricing.

Key strength: Template-driven copy generation for 90+ marketing formats — the fastest way from blank page to draft.
Best for: Marketers, copywriters, and content teams who need to generate large volumes of marketing assets quickly.
Starting price: Free (limited) / Pro from ~$49/month (verify on copy.ai)

+ 90+ copy templates · + Workflow automation · − Not a grammar checker · − Pro tier is pricey
Try Copy.ai Read Review

7. Jasper AI

Best AI writing assistant overall
★★★★★ 4.5/5

Jasper AI is the most full-featured AI content platform in this comparison — covering ideation, first drafts, rewriting, brand voice training, and SEO optimization in a single environment. It's in a different category from Grammarly: rather than correcting what you've written, Jasper writes alongside you, generating blog posts, ad campaigns, social content, and long-form articles from detailed prompts. Teams can train Jasper on their brand voice and style guidelines so all AI-generated output stays on-brand without manual review.

Jasper's pricing positions it as a professional tool — Creator plans start at ~$39/month, with Pro plans at ~$59/month for teams with multiple seats and collaboration features. The positioning reflects its ambition: this is content production infrastructure, not a writing helper. If Grammarly solved a grammar problem, Jasper solves a content volume problem.

Key strength: Brand voice training plus AI drafting at scale — the most complete content production platform in this list.
Best for: Marketing teams, agencies, and content operations that need to produce high volumes of on-brand content efficiently.
Starting price: Creator from ~$39/month (verify on jasper.ai)

+ Brand voice training · + Comprehensive content platform · − No free tier · − Expensive for individuals

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a free alternative to Grammarly?

Yes — LanguageTool offers a free plan that checks grammar, spelling, and style across 25+ languages in your browser. Hemingway Editor's web version is free with no account required and focuses on readability clarity. QuillBot's free tier includes paraphrasing and limited grammar checks. These are genuinely useful for occasional writing tasks, though advanced features like tone adjustment are gated behind paid tiers.

Which Grammarly alternative is best for long-form content?

ProWritingAid is generally regarded as the strongest alternative for long-form writing. It runs 20+ in-depth writing reports covering overused words, sentence length variation, pacing, and dialogue consistency. It integrates directly with Word, Scrivener, and Google Docs. For writers who produce thousands of words per week, ProWritingAid's structural analysis goes well beyond sentence-level grammar checking.

Can QuillBot replace Grammarly?

QuillBot and Grammarly serve different primary use cases. Grammarly excels at catching grammar and tone issues in your existing writing. QuillBot's core strength is paraphrasing — it rewrites sentences in different styles (Standard, Formal, Academic, Creative, Concise) to help you rework content quickly. If you need a grammar checker, QuillBot is a secondary tool; if you need to rephrase existing text, it outperforms Grammarly for that specific task.

What is the cheapest Grammarly alternative?

LanguageTool Premium is among the cheapest paid alternatives, with annual plans working out to under $5/month. For zero-cost use, Hemingway Editor's web version and LanguageTool's free tier both offer meaningful functionality without a subscription. QuillBot also has a free tier with paraphrasing and basic grammar checks that handles light use without payment.
