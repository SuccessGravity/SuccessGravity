⚡ How to Set Up Zapier Automations: A Practical Guide

By David · Founder & Editor · Updated November 2026

✓ Pricing verified · ~15 min read

Disclosure: This article may contain affiliate links. If you buy through them, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Learn more.

Zapier connects more than 7,000 apps and lets you automate the repetitive handoffs between them — no code required. The concept is straightforward: when something happens in App A, do something in App B. But the gap between understanding the concept and having working automations that actually save time is where most new users stall.

This guide covers everything from your first Zap through multi-step workflows with filters, paths, and conditional logic. By the end, you'll have the foundations for automating lead capture, CRM updates, Slack notifications, and routine admin tasks that currently require manual copy-paste.

If you're evaluating whether Zapier is right for your use case — or comparing it against Make, n8n, or Pabbly Connect — start with our Zapier alternatives guide first.

Quick reference

  • Free plan: 100 tasks/month, single-step Zaps only
  • Starter: ~$29.99/mo (annual) — multi-step Zaps, 750 tasks
  • Professional: ~$73.50/mo (annual) — filters, paths, 2,000 tasks
  • Trigger: the event that starts a Zap
  • Action: what Zapier does in response
  • Task: one successful Action (how Zapier bills usage)

Step 1: Create Your Account and Connect Your Apps

Go to zapier.com and sign up with your business email. The free plan is enough to follow this guide. After signup, the first thing to do is connect the apps you plan to automate — Zapier calls this "Connected Accounts."

Navigate to Settings → Connected Accounts and add each app you use. Common starting points: Gmail or Google Workspace, Slack, your CRM (HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive), a form tool (Typeform, Jotform, Google Forms), Airtable or Google Sheets, and your e-commerce platform if applicable (Shopify, WooCommerce, Stripe).

Connecting accounts upfront saves interruptions when you build your first Zap. Each connection requires you to authenticate via OAuth — Zapier opens a browser popup, you log in to the target app, and approve the access. Zapier stores the credentials securely and reuses them for every Zap you build with that app.

Step 2: Build Your First Zap — Choose a Trigger

Click + Create → Zaps from the left sidebar. The Zap editor opens. Every Zap starts with a Trigger — the event that starts the automation.

For your first Zap, start with something simple and high-value: "When a new form submission arrives in Typeform (or Google Forms), add a row to Google Sheets." This covers a real use case, is low-risk to test, and teaches you the core mechanics.

  1. In the Trigger step, search for your form tool (e.g., Typeform).
  2. Select the trigger event: "New Entry".
  3. Connect your Typeform account (or select the already-connected account from Step 1).
  4. Select the specific form you want to trigger from using the dropdown.
  5. Click Test trigger — Zapier pulls recent submissions from that form to use as sample data. If no submissions exist, submit a test entry in your form first, then come back and test again.

You'll see a list of fields from the trigger — name, email, response text, submission date, etc. These become the data you can pass to any subsequent Action step.

Step 3: Add an Action and Map Your Data Fields

Click + Add step (the plus icon below your Trigger). Select the Action app — in this example, Google Sheets.

  1. Choose the Action event: "Create Spreadsheet Row".
  2. Connect your Google account.
  3. Select the Drive, then the Spreadsheet, then the Sheet (tab) you want to write to.
  4. Zapier maps spreadsheet column headers to data fields. Click each field and use the data picker (the blue icon) to insert values from your trigger — e.g., map the "Email" column to the "Email" field from the Typeform submission.

Data mapping is the core skill in Zapier. The data picker shows every field from every previous step — trigger fields plus any Action outputs from earlier steps in a multi-step Zap. Take a few minutes here to map every column you care about rather than just the first one.

Step 4: Test the Zap and Turn It On

Click Test step in the Action. Zapier runs the Action using the sample data from Step 2. If the test passes, check your Google Sheets tab — you should see a new row with the mapped data.

If the test fails, the error message explains why. Common causes: wrong spreadsheet selected, column headers in the spreadsheet don't match what Zapier is looking for, or OAuth permissions need to be refreshed. Fix the issue and test again.

Once the test passes, click Publish Zap. Toggle the Zap to On. From this point, every new form submission automatically creates a row in your spreadsheet without any manual action required.

Important: Zapier's free plan polls for trigger events roughly every 15 minutes. Paid plans reduce this to 2 minutes (Starter) or near-instant via webhooks (Professional+). For time-sensitive automations like lead routing or customer notifications, a paid plan is worth it.

Step 5: Add Filters to Control When the Zap Runs

Filters (available on Professional plans and above) let you add conditions so a Zap only proceeds if certain criteria are met. Without a filter, your Zap runs on every trigger event. With a filter, it runs only when the data matches your rules.

Example: You have a Zap that sends a Slack notification when a new lead is added to your CRM. You only want notifications for leads with an estimated deal value above $5,000, so you don't flood Slack with low-value enquiries.

  1. Between the Trigger and Action steps, click the + icon and select Filter.
  2. Set the condition: "Deal Value (number) → Greater than → 5000".
  3. Zapier will only continue to the Action step when this condition is true. When a trigger fires but the condition isn't met, Zapier stops the Zap for that run (it counts as a filtered run, not a task).

You can combine multiple filter conditions with AND (all must be true) or OR (any can be true) logic. Filters are one of the highest-leverage features in Zapier — they dramatically reduce noise in automations that touch notification channels.

Step 6: Build Multi-Step Zaps for Complex Workflows

Multi-step Zaps (Starter plan and above) allow more than one Action per trigger. The key insight is that each Action's output becomes available as data for the next step, so you can chain operations.

Example multi-step workflow: A new Stripe payment completes → (1) Add customer to Mailchimp list → (2) Create a contact in HubSpot CRM → (3) Send a Slack message to your #sales channel with the customer name and amount.

  1. Build the trigger (Stripe: New Payment Completed).
  2. Add Action 1: Mailchimp → Add/Update Subscriber. Map the email and name fields from the Stripe trigger data.
  3. Add Action 2: HubSpot → Create Contact. Map email, name, and add a property for "Source: Zapier".
  4. Add Action 3: Slack → Send Channel Message. Write your message template, inserting the customer name and payment amount using the data picker.

Multi-step Zaps count one task per successful Action (not per trigger). A three-action Zap uses three tasks per run. This is worth noting when estimating your monthly task usage.

Step 7: Use Paths for Conditional Branching

Paths (Professional plan and above) let you create if/else branching logic in a single Zap, so different trigger conditions route to different Actions. Without Paths, you'd need separate Zaps with duplicate triggers for each condition.

Example: A new contact form submission routes to different team members based on the "Inquiry Type" field — Sales, Support, or Partnership.

  1. Add a Paths by Zapier step after your trigger.
  2. Define Path A: Inquiry Type = "Sales" → Add Actions for the sales workflow (create CRM lead, notify sales Slack channel).
  3. Define Path B: Inquiry Type = "Support" → Add Actions for the support workflow (create Zendesk ticket, send confirmation email).
  4. Define Path C (the catch-all): Everything else → Send to a shared inbox or add to a general tracking sheet.

Each path can have its own filter conditions and action sequences. Tasks are only counted for the paths that actually execute — if a trigger fires and only Path A runs, only Path A's actions count toward your task quota.

Step 8: Monitor Your Zap History and Task Usage

Go to Zap History in the left sidebar. This shows every Zap run with its status: success, filtered, error, or held. For errors, the history shows exactly which step failed and why — the error log is specific enough to diagnose most issues without contacting support.

Practical monitoring habits:

  • Check Zap History once a week when you're new, then monthly once automations are stable.
  • Set up email notifications for Zap errors in Settings → Notifications so you're alerted when something breaks.
  • Review your task usage under Account → Task Usage ahead of your billing cycle. If you're regularly near the limit, either optimize your Zaps (add filters to reduce unnecessary runs) or upgrade your plan.

5 Practical Zapier Automations Worth Building

These are common, high-value automations across most business types:

  1. Lead capture to CRM: New form submission (Typeform/Jotform) → Create contact in CRM (HubSpot/Pipedrive) + notify sales team in Slack. Eliminates manual lead entry and ensures immediate visibility.
  2. New customer onboarding: Stripe payment completed → Add to email marketing list (Mailchimp/ActiveCampaign) + create project in Asana/ClickUp + send welcome Slack DM. Triggers the full onboarding sequence from a single payment event.
  3. Social proof collection: New 5-star review in Google Business Profile or Trustpilot → Add to Airtable testimonial database + post to Slack #marketing channel. Builds your testimonial library automatically.
  4. Calendar to CRM logging: New Google Calendar event created with a specific keyword in the title → Log meeting in CRM with date, subject, and attendee. Keeps activity history current without manual input.
  5. E-commerce order tracking: New Shopify order → Add row to Google Sheets with order details + send SMS to fulfillment team via Twilio. Gives your team real-time visibility into new orders across channels.

When to Consider a Zapier Alternative

Zapier is the right choice for most teams: the app library is the largest, the interface is the most beginner-friendly, and the reliability is proven. But it's worth considering alternatives in these situations:

  • High task volume at lower cost: Make (formerly Integromat) prices by operations rather than tasks, and handles complex multi-step workflows with a visual canvas that's more transparent than Zapier's linear editor. For automations with 10,000+ monthly operations, Make is significantly cheaper.
  • Open-source / self-hosted: n8n is fully open-source and can run on your own server with no per-task fees. It has a steeper setup curve but no usage limits and no vendor lock-in.
  • Lifetime deal / one-time cost: Pabbly Connect offers a lifetime deal that makes it cost-effective for teams with predictable, ongoing automation needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does Zapier cost?

Zapier's free plan includes 100 tasks per month with single-step Zaps. The Starter plan (~$29.99/month billed annually) adds multi-step Zaps and 750 tasks. Professional (~$73.50/month billed annually) adds filters, paths, and 2,000+ tasks. For teams, the Team plan adds shared workspaces and starts at ~$103.50/month. Confirm current rates on Zapier's pricing page — these figures are accurate as of November 2026 but may change.

What's the difference between a Trigger and an Action in Zapier?

A Trigger is the event that starts a Zap — something that happens in one app (a form submission, a new email, a Stripe payment). An Action is what Zapier does in response — sends a message, creates a record, adds a row to a spreadsheet. Every Zap has exactly one Trigger and at least one Action; multi-step Zaps have multiple Actions.

What are Zapier alternatives?

The main alternatives are Make (formerly Integromat) for complex multi-step workflows with a visual editor and lower cost per operation; n8n for open-source, self-hosted automation with no task limits; and Pabbly Connect for a one-time lifetime deal. See our full roundup at successgravity.com/alternatives/zapier/.

How do I test a Zap without sending real data?

Every Zap has a "Test" step before you publish. You can use test data (sample records Zapier pulls from your trigger app) to verify the flow end to end before turning it on. For sensitive workflows, Zapier also lets you turn a Zap off, run it manually on a single trigger event, and inspect the history before enabling it for live traffic.
