🔍 How to Do an SEO Audit with Ahrefs (2026 Step-by-Step Guide)

By David · Founder & Editor, Success Gravity · Updated November 2026

✓ Last updated: November 2026 · Pricing verified

Most websites have SEO problems they don't know about — broken links that bleed ranking equity, pages with duplicate title tags that confuse Google, content ranking on page two for keywords that could be on page one with minor fixes. An SEO audit surfaces these problems and turns them into a prioritized action list. Ahrefs is the most comprehensive tool available for this work, covering technical site health, backlink analysis, and keyword research in a single platform.

This guide walks through a complete SEO audit using Ahrefs: from setting up the crawl through identifying and prioritizing technical issues, analyzing your backlink profile, and finding keyword opportunities your competitors have that you don't. The process works for businesses running their first audit and teams doing quarterly checkups on a site they actively manage.

Step 1: Set Up Your Ahrefs Account and Add Your Site

Ahrefs' pricing page lists plans starting at ~$129/month for the Lite tier, which covers one user and basic crawl and backlink access. The Standard plan (~$249/mo) is better suited for active SEO work because it includes more historical data and higher crawl limits. If cost is a concern, Ahrefs Webmaster Tools (free for verified site owners) provides limited access to Site Audit and backlink data — worth using as a starting point before committing to a paid subscription.

Once your account is active, navigate to Site Audit and click + New project. Enter your domain and configure the crawl settings: scope (entire domain vs. a specific subfolder), crawl speed (adjust lower for smaller hosting plans), and whether to crawl JavaScript-rendered pages. For most small to medium business sites, the default settings work well. Connect your Google Search Console to the project so Ahrefs can pull in your actual search performance data alongside its crawl findings — this combination is significantly more useful than either source alone.

Step 2: Run Your First Crawl and Review Your Health Score

Trigger the crawl and wait for it to complete — this can take anywhere from a few minutes to several hours depending on site size. Once done, the main Site Audit dashboard shows your Health Score, a percentage calculated by dividing pages with no critical issues by the total crawled pages. According to Ahrefs' documentation, a score above 90 is good, 70–90 is moderate, and below 70 suggests significant issues impacting search performance.

The dashboard organizes findings into three severity levels: Errors (critical issues that directly harm crawlability and rankings), Warnings (issues that reduce efficiency or quality), and Notices (informational items worth reviewing but rarely urgent). Focus all your attention on Errors first. Common critical errors include: broken internal links (404s), pages blocked by robots.txt that shouldn't be, missing or duplicate title tags, missing meta descriptions, and redirect chains that waste crawl budget.

The Top Issues view is the most efficient starting point: it ranks all detected issues by the number of affected pages, so you can see at a glance whether you have one broken link or three hundred. For each issue type, Ahrefs provides an explanation of why it matters and a recommended fix — which is valuable if you're doing this for the first time.

Step 3: Fix Critical Technical Issues

Work through Errors in order of affected page count. The highest-impact fixes for most sites fall into three categories:

Broken internal links (4xx errors): These waste crawl budget and create a poor user experience. For each broken internal URL, either update the link to point to the correct destination or add a 301 redirect from the broken URL to the right page. Ahrefs shows you exactly which pages contain each broken link, making fixes straightforward to track.

Missing, duplicate, or truncated title tags and meta descriptions: Title tags are one of the strongest on-page ranking signals. Ahrefs flags pages with no title tag, duplicate titles (identical across multiple pages), and titles over 60 characters that will be truncated in search results. Each of these is a quick CMS fix with direct SEO impact — especially for sites that have grown over time without consistent editorial standards.

Slow page load times and Core Web Vitals issues: Ahrefs flags pages with performance problems detected during crawl. For deeper page speed analysis, the Performance report within Site Audit shows Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS), and First Input Delay (FID) data pulled from your connected Google Search Console. Pages failing Core Web Vitals thresholds are worth prioritizing, particularly your highest-traffic landing pages where speed directly influences bounce rate and conversion.

After working through the Errors list, move to Warnings. Common high-value warnings include: low word count pages (thin content that may not rank well), images missing alt text (both a ranking and accessibility issue), and pages with too many internal links on a single page (which dilutes link equity).

Step 4: Analyze Backlinks and Find Link Opportunities

Open Site Explorer and enter your domain. The Backlink Profile overview shows your total referring domains, Domain Rating (DR — Ahrefs' authority metric for your domain), and the trend over time. A rising DR with a growing count of referring domains from diverse, relevant sources indicates a healthy link profile. A declining referring domain count or sudden DR drop may indicate link loss or a manual penalty.

Navigate to Referring domains and filter by DR > 30 to focus on authoritative links worth protecting. Check for lost backlinks in the Lost tab — these are pages that previously linked to you but no longer do. For high-value lost links (from authoritative domains), it's worth investigating whether the linking page was removed (and whether a redirect or outreach could recover the link) or whether the link was replaced by a competitor.

The Link Intersect tool (under Competitive Analysis) is particularly valuable for link building. Enter two or three competitors' domains and your own, and Ahrefs shows all the domains that link to your competitors but not to you. These are pre-qualified link prospects — sites that have already shown willingness to link to your type of content. Ahrefs' documentation describes this as one of the fastest ways to build a targeted link prospecting list.

Also review your Anchors report. An unnatural anchor profile — where the same keyword phrase appears as the anchor text on a high percentage of your inbound links — can be a manual penalty signal. Healthy profiles show a mix of branded anchors ("Success Gravity"), bare URLs, generic terms ("click here"), and some keyword anchors.

Step 5: Research Keywords and Find Content Gaps

The final stage of the audit shifts from fixing existing problems to identifying growth opportunities. In Site Explorer, navigate to Organic Keywords for your domain. Sort by position to see where you rank. Focus on positions 5–20 — pages ranking on the second half of page one or top of page two for significant keywords. These are your highest-ROI content optimization targets: a page already ranking on page two has demonstrated topical relevance and needs relatively minor improvements (more comprehensive content, better internal linking, or stronger on-page optimization) to move to page one.

Next, run a Content Gap analysis. Navigate to Competitive Analysis → Content Gap, enter two or three direct competitors, and Ahrefs will show every keyword they rank for that you don't. Filter by keyword difficulty (KD) below 30 to find attainable targets — keywords where you can realistically compete without an enormous link-building campaign. These are your new content opportunities: topics your competitors have covered that you haven't, with existing search demand already proven by their rankings.

Keywords Explorer lets you dig deeper on any individual keyword: volume, KD, clicks per search (some queries answer themselves in SERPs without click-through), and the SERP overview showing what format is currently winning (long-form article, listicle, product page, tool, or video). Matching your content format to what's already winning in the SERP is one of the most reliable on-page strategies according to Ahrefs' own SEO research blog.

💡 David's tip: Start with the top 20 issues by affected page count — fixing everything at once leads to analysis paralysis. A focused sprint on the 20 highest-impact fixes will move your Health Score faster than spending the same time working through 200 lower-priority items. After each sprint, re-crawl to track progress and reprioritize.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a paid Ahrefs plan to do an SEO audit?

For a full site crawl and backlink analysis, yes — Ahrefs' Site Audit and Site Explorer features require a paid plan. According to Ahrefs' pricing page, plans start at ~$129/month (Lite). Ahrefs Webmaster Tools is a limited free option that covers basic backlink data and some Site Audit functionality for verified site owners, but the crawl limits and data depth are significantly lower than a paid subscription.

How long does an Ahrefs SEO audit take?

The crawl itself can take anywhere from a few minutes (small sites under 500 pages) to several hours for large sites with tens of thousands of URLs. Reviewing the results, prioritizing issues, and planning fixes is typically a 2–4 hour process for a medium-sized site. Ahrefs' documentation recommends running monthly crawls to track progress on fixes.

What is a good Ahrefs Health Score?

According to Ahrefs' documentation, a Health Score above 90 is considered good. Scores between 70–90 indicate issues worth addressing. Below 70 suggests significant technical problems that are likely impacting search performance. The score is calculated based on the ratio of internal pages with critical issues to the total crawled pages.

What's the difference between Ahrefs Site Audit and Site Explorer?

Site Audit is Ahrefs' technical SEO crawler — it spiders your own website to find on-page and technical issues like broken links, missing meta tags, slow pages, and duplicate content. Site Explorer is for competitive intelligence and backlink analysis — it draws from Ahrefs' external index to show who links to any domain, what keywords a site ranks for, and what competitors are doing. An SEO audit uses both: Site Audit for internal technical issues, Site Explorer for backlink health and competitive keyword gaps.
