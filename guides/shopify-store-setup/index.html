🛒 How to Set Up a Shopify Store in 2026 (Step-by-Step)

By David · Founder & Editor, Success Gravity · Updated October 2026

Shopify is the most widely used e-commerce platform for a reason — it handles the infrastructure that would otherwise require a development team: hosting, SSL, payment processing, inventory, shipping integrations, and checkout. The question we hear most often from people starting out is not "should I use Shopify?" but "what exactly should I do first, and in what order?" That ordering matters more than most tutorials acknowledge. Setting up payments before you've added products means a working payment system connected to nothing.

This guide covers the five steps that actually get a store open and ready to sell, in the order that makes each next step easier. It's written for someone starting from zero — no existing store, no prior Shopify experience — but even if you've started a trial and poked around, the sections on product listing optimisation and payment configuration are worth reading before you launch.

Step 1: Start your trial and choose your plan

Go to shopify.com and start a free trial — no credit card required. Shopify currently offers a 3-day free trial and, on qualifying plans, a promotional first month at reduced pricing (confirm the current offer on Shopify's pricing page, as this changes). During the trial you can build your entire store before being charged anything.

Before committing to a plan, it helps to understand what the plan tiers actually affect. All plans include hosting, SSL, unlimited products, and 24/7 support. The main differences are transaction fees and staff accounts. Basic ($29/month billed monthly, $19/month billed annually) charges 2% on third-party payment transactions; the Shopify plan ($79/month, $49/mo annually) drops that to 1%; Advanced ($299/month) drops it to 0.5% and adds advanced reporting. For a new store using Shopify Payments (which eliminates transaction fees entirely on all plans), Basic is sufficient until you're generating enough revenue that the reporting and account features of higher plans pay for themselves. According to Shopify's help centre, Shopify Payments is available in the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and several other markets — if you're in an unsupported country, factor transaction fees into your plan choice from the start.

What we think is most often underweighted by new store owners is the annual billing discount. Paying $19/month instead of $29/month saves $120/year on Basic — meaningful early on. If you're confident enough to commit for a year, lock it in at signup.

Step 2: Pick a theme and customise your storefront

In your Shopify admin, go to Online Store → Themes. Shopify's Theme Store offers both free and paid themes. For most new stores, starting with a free theme is the right call — the free themes are fast, well-maintained, and conversion-tested. Dawn is Shopify's flagship free theme and the default for good reason: it's clean, mobile-first, and handles a wide range of product types well. Craft is worth considering if your store will lean heavily on brand storytelling and lifestyle imagery.

Paid themes (typically $180–$380 one-time) like Prestige, Impulse, and Symmetry are worth evaluating if you want more built-in section flexibility without apps. However, the honest answer is that theme choice matters far less than most people expect — a well-photographed product on a free theme converts better than a poorly photographed one on a premium theme. The theme editor (accessed via Customize) lets you adjust colours, fonts, header layout, and homepage sections without any code. Spend an hour here rather than a day — the goal is "professional and fast-loading," not "unique and visually complex."

The most important setting to verify: in Theme settings → Colors, set your brand's primary colour for buttons. That single change makes the store feel intentional rather than default, and it takes 30 seconds.

Step 3: Add your products (with optimised listings)

Go to Products → Add product. Each product needs a title, description, images, and pricing. This is where most new store owners underinvest — and it's the single step that most directly affects whether visitors buy. A few specific things that matter more than they appear to:

  • Product title: Write it the way a customer would search for it, not the way you'd describe it internally. "Women's Merino Wool Crew-Neck Sweater" will outperform "Cosy Cloud Tee" in search and in scanning.
  • Images: At minimum, one clean white-background shot and one lifestyle shot. According to Shopify's own merchant research, product pages with multiple image angles see significantly higher add-to-cart rates. Use a minimum of 1000px on the long edge so zoom works properly.
  • Description: Lead with the benefit, then cover the specifics (materials, dimensions, care instructions). The description shows up in Google — write it for a person who hasn't seen the product before, not for someone who already knows what it is.
  • Search engine listing: At the bottom of each product page, edit the SEO title and meta description. The default (your product title + store name) is acceptable; a custom title targeting a search phrase is better.

For collections, create them before you add more than five or six products — it's far easier to organise from the start. Go to Products → Collections → Create collection and either manually assign products or set automated rules (for example, all products tagged "summer" auto-add to a Summer Collection).

Step 4: Configure payments, shipping, and taxes

This is the step that requires the most settings, and where skipping something creates problems after launch rather than before.

Payments: Go to Settings → Payments. If Shopify Payments is available in your country, enable it — it eliminates transaction fees and activates Shop Pay, which meaningfully improves checkout conversion on mobile. You'll need to verify your identity and provide banking details. Add PayPal as a secondary option, as a significant portion of shoppers prefer it. Apple Pay and Google Pay activate automatically through Shopify Payments once enabled.

Shipping: Go to Settings → Shipping and delivery. Create at least one shipping zone covering your primary market and one rate (free shipping over a threshold, flat rate, or carrier-calculated). The most conversion-friendly approach for small stores is a free shipping threshold — Shopify's own data shows it consistently outperforms flat-rate shipping on average order value. A common starting point: free shipping over $50, with a flat-rate $5.99 below it. Adjust once you've run some orders and know your average margin.

Taxes: Shopify calculates taxes automatically based on your business location and where you're shipping to. In Settings → Taxes and duties, verify your business tax registration details are correct. For US merchants, Shopify uses its own tax service (Shopify Tax) to calculate correct state and county rates automatically on eligible plans — consult a tax professional if you have questions about nexus requirements.

Step 5: Connect your domain and go live

Your store starts with a .myshopify.com URL. Before you share it with anyone, connect a real domain. Go to Settings → Domains. You can either buy a domain through Shopify (typically $14–$20/year for a .com) — which auto-connects and renews — or connect an existing domain from a registrar like Namecheap or Cloudflare by updating the DNS A record and CNAME as instructed. Shopify provides specific step-by-step instructions for every major registrar it supports.

Before going live, run through this checklist: place a test order using Shopify's Bogus Gateway (Settings → Payments → Enable test mode) to confirm the checkout flow end-to-end; check your store on a mobile phone (most first visits will be mobile); verify your legal pages are in place (Shopify generates a Privacy Policy and Terms of Service template under Settings → Policies — edit them to match your business); and confirm your email notifications are set up correctly in Settings → Notifications so customers receive order confirmations.

When you're ready, go to Online Store → Preferences and confirm the password protection is turned off. That's it — your store is live. The most valuable next step is connecting Shopify to Google Analytics 4 (our GA4 setup guide) and Google Search Console (our GSC guide) so you can see where traffic comes from and which products get search impressions.

💡 David's tip: Don't spend more than two hours on your theme before adding products. The thing that determines whether a store succeeds is whether the products are compelling — the storefront is packaging. Get your first five products live and test them with real traffic before investing in design polish.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does Shopify cost per month?

Shopify's Basic plan starts at $29/month (billed monthly) or $19/month billed annually. The standard Shopify plan is $79/month ($49/mo annually) and Advanced is $299/month. All plans include hosting, SSL, and unlimited products. There is a 3-day free trial — confirm current promotional pricing on Shopify's pricing page, as it changes.

Can I use my own domain name with Shopify?

Yes. You can either buy a domain directly through Shopify (which auto-connects it) or connect an existing domain from a registrar like Namecheap, GoDaddy, or Cloudflare. Shopify provides step-by-step DNS instructions for any registrar it supports.

Do I need a business license to sell on Shopify?

Shopify doesn't require one to open a store. Whether your local jurisdiction requires a business license depends on where you're based and what you sell. Most sole proprietors start selling first and formalise once the business is generating real revenue — check local requirements for your specific situation.

What's the best Shopify theme for a new store?

Dawn (free) is Shopify's flagship free theme and a solid default for most product types. Craft (free) works better for stores leading with brand storytelling. For paid themes, Prestige and Impulse are consistently recommended for conversion performance. The most important factor is that the theme is fast and mobile-optimised — Shopify's Theme Store shows Core Web Vitals scores for each.
