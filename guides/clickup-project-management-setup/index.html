📚 How to Set Up ClickUp for Project Management (Step-by-Step 2026 Guide)

By David · Founder & Editor, Success Gravity · ✓ Last updated: November 2026 · Pricing verified

Disclosure: This guide may contain affiliate links. If you buy through them, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Learn more.

ClickUp promises a lot — "one app to replace them all" is its tagline — and the honest answer is that it delivers on that promise once you invest in setting it up properly. The platform spans task management, documentation, time tracking, dashboards, whiteboards, and native automations in a single subscription. The challenge is that this breadth comes with a hierarchy and a vocabulary that takes a few hours to understand before the payoff becomes clear.

The good news is that ClickUp's Free Forever plan is genuinely functional for most small teams — unlimited tasks, unlimited members, and access to the core views, according to ClickUp's pricing page. This guide covers the five setup steps that take a new ClickUp workspace from a blank default to a working project management system: building the right Space and Folder structure, configuring your first project with custom fields, creating the views your team will actually live in, and wiring up the automations that keep the system running without manual housekeeping.

ClickUp hierarchy: Workspace → Space → Folder → List → Task. Lists are where tasks live; Folders group Lists for one project area.

Step 1: Create Your ClickUp Workspace and Choose a Plan

When you sign up at clickup.com, ClickUp creates your first workspace and walks you through an onboarding flow asking about your team's main use case. This is helpful for generating starter templates, but the real setup begins after you dismiss the wizard. Name your workspace after your company or project — this appears in the URL and in the browser tab, so a clear name matters if you have multiple workspaces.

For most teams starting out, the Free Forever plan is the right starting point. According to ClickUp's pricing page, it includes unlimited tasks, unlimited members, 100MB storage, and 100 automation runs per month — enough to run a real project workflow and evaluate whether the paid features are worth the upgrade. The Unlimited plan at $7/member/month removes automation limits, adds unlimited storage, and unlocks integrations with more external services. The Business plan at $12/member/month adds advanced dashboards, time tracking with rounding rules, and more granular permission controls for larger teams. Pay monthly until you're sure the tool fits your workflow; the annual discount (typically around 30%) is worth it once you're committed.

Invite your team members from Settings → People. ClickUp's permission model lets you assign roles: Owner, Admin, Member, and Guest. Members can create and edit content; Guests get read-only or comment-only access to specific Spaces — useful for clients or contractors who need visibility without edit rights.

Step 2: Set Up Your Space and Folder Structure

Spaces are the top-level organizing unit inside a ClickUp workspace — think of them as departments or functional areas. Common Space names for small businesses include Marketing, Product / Development, Operations, Client Work, and Admin. Create a Space for each major area of work by clicking the + icon next to "Spaces" in the left sidebar. Keep the initial number small — three to five Spaces is right for most teams under 20 people. You can add more later, but starting with fewer Spaces keeps the sidebar navigable.

Inside each Space, Folders group related projects. A Marketing Space might have Folders for Q4 2026 Campaigns, Content Calendar, and SEO Projects. Folders are optional — for very small teams, working directly in Spaces without Folders is fine — but for anyone juggling more than four or five concurrent projects in one area, Folders prevent the sidebar from becoming a scrollable mess of orphaned Lists. Create a Folder by right-clicking a Space name and choosing New Folder.

The rule of thumb: use Spaces to separate teams or major function areas, use Folders to group projects within those areas, and reserve Lists (the next level down) for the actual project work. This maps to how most teams already think about their work — department, then project, then tasks — making the hierarchy intuitive for new team members to navigate without a training session.

Step 3: Create Your First Project List and Configure Custom Fields

A List is the working unit where tasks actually live. Create a List inside a Folder (or directly in a Space if you're not using Folders) by clicking + New List. Name it after the project — "November Product Launch," "Blog Content Q4," or "Client A Onboarding." Inside the List, each row is a task. The default task view shows the task name, status, assignee, and due date, which is enough to get started.

Custom Fields are what separate a basic task list from a genuinely useful project management tool. ClickUp lets you add custom fields to any List to capture data that matters for your specific workflow. Useful fields to add for most projects include: Priority (dropdown: Urgent / High / Normal / Low), Effort (dropdown or number: hours estimated), Sprint (text or dropdown for teams doing agile cycles), Client (text or relation to a People field), and Blocked By (a Relationship field linking to another task). Navigate to List Settings → Custom Fields to add them. Custom fields added at the List level apply only to that List; custom fields added at the Space level apply to every List inside that Space — useful for fields like Priority that you want consistently available everywhere.

Set up your Status workflow next. ClickUp's default statuses are To Do, In Progress, and Complete, but you can customize them per List. A content production workflow might use: Brief, Writing, Editing, Design, Scheduled, Published. A software development List might use: Backlog, In Sprint, In Review, Testing, Done. Right-click any status in the task view or go to List Settings to edit the status names and colours. Consistent statuses are what make automation (covered in Step 5) reliable.

Step 4: Build Your Views — Board, Calendar, Gantt, and List

One of ClickUp's genuine strengths is the ability to look at the same tasks through multiple view types without duplicating data. All views show the same underlying tasks; switching between them just changes how you visualize the work. Create a new view by clicking + Add View at the top of any List or Folder.

The views worth setting up for most project teams are: List view (the default — spreadsheet-style, best for bulk editing and sorting by any field), Board view (Kanban-style columns grouped by Status — best for daily stand-ups and spotting work in progress), Calendar view (tasks laid out by Due Date — best for deadline management and spotting deadline clusters), and Gantt view (Unlimited plan and above — timeline bars with dependency lines, best for planning project phases and communicating timelines to stakeholders). Each view can be saved with filters and sort orders that persist, so a "My Tasks" Board view filtered to Assignee = Me can sit permanently in the sidebar for personal daily planning without affecting the team's shared view.

For teams managing client work, a Table view at the Folder level — showing all tasks across multiple project Lists in one scrollable table — gives a cross-project status overview that the sidebar alone cannot provide. Add it to a Folder to see everything in that client's area at once.

💡 David's tip: Save a Board view at the Space level — not just inside one List — filtered by Assignee and sorted by Due Date. This gives every team member a single "my work across all projects" board they can check each morning without hunting through individual project Lists. In ClickUp, a view at the Space or Folder level aggregates tasks from every List inside it. It takes two minutes to set up and becomes the default daily dashboard for most team members once they discover it.

Step 5: Automate Routine Tasks and Connect Integrations

ClickUp's built-in Automations (accessible via Automations in the sidebar or inside any List's settings) let you define trigger-action rules that run automatically. The most useful automations for getting started are: When Status changes to "Done" → Assign to reviewer (routes finished work to the next person without manual hand-off), When Due Date passes and Status is not "Done" → Change Priority to Urgent (flags overdue tasks automatically), and When task is created in this List → Apply template (pre-fills new tasks with standard checklists). The Free plan allows 100 automation runs per month — enough for small teams. The Unlimited plan removes the cap.

For integrations, ClickUp connects natively to Slack (sends task update notifications to a channel), Google Drive (attach Drive files directly to tasks), GitHub (link commits and pull requests to tasks for development teams), Zoom (start Zoom calls from inside tasks), and dozens of other tools. Set up the Slack integration first if your team uses it — getting ClickUp notifications into Slack channels your team already monitors drives adoption faster than any other single integration. Install integrations from Settings → Integrations.

For anything not covered by native integrations, ClickUp connects to Zapier and Make (formerly Integromat), which unlocks connections to thousands of additional apps. Common Zapier workflows include: creating a ClickUp task automatically when a new lead comes in from a form, syncing ClickUp task completions to a Google Sheet, or creating a ClickUp task from a new Stripe payment — useful for service businesses that need to trigger onboarding workflows from purchase events.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ClickUp free for small teams?

Yes. ClickUp's Free Forever plan includes unlimited tasks, unlimited members, 100MB of storage, and access to List, Board, and Calendar views. The main restrictions are 100 automation runs per month and limited Dashboard widgets. The Unlimited plan at $7/member/month removes the automation cap and adds unlimited storage. According to ClickUp's pricing page, the free plan has no time limit.

How is ClickUp different from Asana or Trello?

ClickUp has a deeper hierarchy (Workspace → Space → Folder → List → Task) than Trello's simple board-column-card or Asana's project-section-task. This makes ClickUp more flexible but more complex to set up. The key differentiator is breadth: ClickUp includes built-in Docs, Goals, Dashboards, Time Tracking, Whiteboards, and Automations — features that require separate subscriptions in Asana or are absent from Trello. Teams that want one tool to replace Trello, Google Docs, and a time tracker often choose ClickUp.

How do I migrate from Asana or Trello to ClickUp?

ClickUp offers built-in import tools for Asana, Trello, Jira, Monday.com, and several other platforms. Go to Workspace Settings → Import/Export, choose your source tool, and authenticate. ClickUp maps tasks, assignees, due dates, and attachments automatically. Custom fields may not transfer perfectly — review the imported data and adjust manually after import. Running a test migration on a single project before migrating everything is the approach ClickUp's own help center recommends.

What is the difference between a Space, Folder, and List in ClickUp?

Spaces are the highest-level containers — typically representing teams or departments. Folders group related projects within a Space. Lists are the working level where actual tasks live. This four-level hierarchy (Space → Folder → List → Task) gives ClickUp its flexibility: a small team might use just Spaces and Lists, while a larger organization adds Folders to group multiple active projects without the sidebar becoming unmanageable.
