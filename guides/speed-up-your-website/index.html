⚡ Make Your Website Faster This Afternoon (Free Tools Only)

By David · Founder & Editor · Updated September 2026

Slow sites lose twice: visitors abandon them, and Google quietly ranks them lower. The good news is that most small-business sites are slow for boring, fixable reasons — usually enormous images and no caching — and every tool you need to fix them today is free.

This is the exact triage I run on any site that feels sluggish, in the order that delivers the most speed per minute of effort.

Illustration by Success Gravity — a simplified view of the setup flow, not a product screenshot.

Step 1: Measure first — and read the right number

Run your homepage through PageSpeed Insights. Ignore the score bubble for a moment and look at the opportunities list — it names your specific problems in order of impact. Also note the Core Web Vitals assessment; “LCP” (how fast the main content appears) is the one visitors feel most.

Step 2: Fix images — the usual villain

Nine times out of ten, the biggest opportunity says images. Run your hero and product images through TinyPNG — it routinely cuts 60–80% of file size with no visible difference. While you're at it: never upload a 4000-pixel photo to fill a 800-pixel slot; resize before uploading. This step alone transforms most slow sites.

Step 3: Put Cloudflare's free plan in front

Cloudflare's free tier caches your site on servers worldwide, so visitors load it from somewhere nearby instead of your origin host, and adds free SSL and DDoS protection. Setup means pointing your domain's nameservers at Cloudflare — about 15 minutes, and their wizard walks you through it. For most shared-hosting sites this is the single biggest infrastructure win available at $0.

Step 4: Trim scripts you forgot you added

Old chat widgets, duplicate analytics, unused fonts, that popup tool you tried once — each one taxes every page load. Audit your site head, keep what you use, delete the rest. Loading fonts? Two families maximum, with only the weights you actually use.

Step 5: Re-measure, then stop

Run PageSpeed again and compare. If you moved from red into orange-green territory and the site feels fast on your own phone over cellular data, stop — chasing a perfect 100 has terrible returns. Re-check quarterly or after redesigns; speed regressions sneak in through new images and widgets, not through mystery.

💡 David's tip: Test on your phone over mobile data, not your office Wi-Fi. That's how most of your visitors actually experience the site — and it keeps your optimism honest.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's a 'good' PageSpeed score?

Aim for green Core Web Vitals rather than a specific score. Mobile scores in the 70–90 range with passing vitals put you ahead of most competitors; a perfect 100 is rarely worth the engineering time.

Will faster hosting fix everything?

Better hosting raises the floor but can't outrun 8 MB images. Do the free fixes first; upgrade hosting when your origin response time (TTFB) stays slow after them.

Does speed really affect Google rankings?

Yes — Core Web Vitals are a confirmed ranking signal. It's a tiebreaker rather than a dominant factor, but the visitor-behavior effects (bounce, conversion) are large and immediate.

GTmetrix vs PageSpeed Insights?

Use both: PageSpeed for Google's official view and field data, GTmetrix for waterfall detail showing exactly which file slows you down, plus scheduled monitoring on the free plan.
