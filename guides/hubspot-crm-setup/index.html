How to Set Up HubSpot CRM for Free in 2026

By David · Founder & Editor, Success Gravity · ✓ Last updated: October 2026 · Pricing verified

Disclosure: This page may contain affiliate links. We may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Learn more.

HubSpot CRM is completely free for unlimited users — no credit card, no trial clock, no hidden limit on contacts. For small businesses that have been managing their pipeline in a spreadsheet or chasing leads across email threads, the upgrade to a proper CRM is one of the highest-ROI changes you can make, and HubSpot's free plan removes every financial barrier to starting. The platform covers the full contact-to-close workflow: storing contact records, tracking email opens, managing deal stages, scheduling meetings, and logging every interaction automatically.

This guide covers the five steps to go from a blank HubSpot account to actively tracking deals: creating the account, customizing your pipeline, importing contacts, connecting email, and logging your first deal. Most teams complete this in under an hour. HubSpot CRM is the best free starting point for small business CRM in 2026 — not because it's the most feature-rich option at the free tier, but because its combination of ease of use, integration breadth, and a clear upgrade path makes it the lowest-risk way to get your team using a CRM for the first time.

Step 1: Create Your Free HubSpot Account

Go to hubspot.com/crm → and click Get started free. Sign up with your work email address — using a work email (rather than Gmail or Yahoo) is worth the small friction because it allows HubSpot to pre-fill your company name and connect your organization's domain from the start. When prompted to select your primary product, choose CRM. You'll also be asked about your company size and industry, which HubSpot uses to personalize your getting-started recommendations.

HubSpot's free plan is comprehensive. According to HubSpot's pricing page, the free CRM includes: unlimited users, up to 1,000,000 contacts, 1 deal pipeline with up to 1 million deals, email tracking (200 notifications per month), meeting scheduling links (1 meeting link per user), live chat (1 inbox), and basic contact, deal, and task reporting. There's no credit card required and no time limit — this is a permanent free tier, not a trial. For teams evaluating CRM for the first time, the free plan is sufficient to run your pipeline for months before any paid upgrade is worth considering.

Step 2: Set Up Your Deal Pipeline

Navigate to Sales → Pipelines in the top navigation. HubSpot creates a default pipeline with standard stages: Appointment Scheduled, Qualified to Buy, Presentation Scheduled, Decision Maker Bought-In, Contract Sent, Closed Won, and Closed Lost. For most small businesses, this default set needs renaming to match how your business actually sells. Click Edit pipeline and rename each stage to reflect your real sales process — for example, a consulting firm might use: Initial Contact, Discovery Call, Proposal Sent, Negotiation, Closed Won, Closed Lost. A SaaS company might prefer: Lead, Free Trial Started, Trial Active, Proposal, Closed Won.

Each stage can be assigned a probability (0–100%) that HubSpot uses to calculate weighted pipeline value in your reports — update these percentages to reflect your actual historical close rates for each stage if you have that data. You can also add required properties to specific stages: for example, requiring a budget amount to be filled in before a deal can advance to Proposal Sent. This enforces data completeness and makes your pipeline reports more accurate. The free plan supports one pipeline; the Starter plan unlocks additional pipelines for teams with multiple product lines or different sales motions.

Step 3: Import Your Contacts

Navigate to Contacts → Import in the top navigation and select Import a file. HubSpot accepts CSV files and Excel spreadsheets. If your contacts currently live in a spreadsheet, export them to CSV first. On the import screen, HubSpot walks you through mapping your spreadsheet columns to HubSpot properties — for example, mapping your "Company" column to HubSpot's "Company Name" property, and your "Phone" column to "Phone Number." HubSpot's standard contact properties cover the common fields; if you track custom information (like a customer type or region), you can create custom contact properties in Settings → Properties before importing.

HubSpot's deduplication runs automatically during import: if an imported email address already exists in your account, HubSpot merges the new data with the existing record rather than creating a duplicate. After the import completes, review the summary screen — it will show how many contacts were created, how many were updated (matched existing records), and how many had errors. Download the error file and correct any records that failed to import (typically caused by malformed email addresses or phone numbers in unexpected formats). For larger imports, HubSpot also supports importing companies and deals alongside contacts in a single operation using a multi-file import.

Step 4: Connect Your Email

Navigate to Settings → General → Email and click Connect personal email. HubSpot supports Gmail and Google Workspace, Outlook, and Office 365. The connection uses OAuth — you authorize HubSpot to access your inbox without entering your password in HubSpot. Once connected, HubSpot installs a browser extension (for Chrome) or an Outlook add-in that adds a HubSpot panel directly in your email client, showing you the contact record for whoever you're emailing.

Email tracking works in both directions. Emails you send from your connected inbox get a tracking pixel automatically — HubSpot notifies you (up to 200 times per month on the free plan) when a recipient opens your email. The BCC logging address in your HubSpot settings allows any email you send from any device to be logged to the relevant contact record — just BCC that address and the email appears in the contact's activity timeline automatically. This is useful for emails sent from mobile or from email clients that don't have the HubSpot extension installed. For outbound prospecting emails, the Log and track checkbox in the HubSpot sidebar within Gmail lets you control tracking on a per-email basis.

Step 5: Track Your First Deal

Navigate to Sales → Deals and click Create deal in the upper right. Give the deal a name (typically the company name + what you're selling, e.g. "Acme Corp — Annual Contract"), select the pipeline stage it currently sits in, enter the deal amount and expected close date, and associate the deal with a contact and company. Associating all three — deal, contact, and company — is important because it connects the deal to the full contact and company history: every email, call, meeting, and note linked to that contact will appear in the deal record's activity timeline.

Once the deal is created, move it through the pipeline by dragging it in the Kanban board view or by changing the stage in the deal record. Set a Close date — HubSpot uses this to calculate pipeline value over time and to surface overdue deals. Create a Task directly from the deal record (the Tasks panel on the right sidebar) to schedule your next follow-up action: a call, an email, a proposal delivery. HubSpot will show overdue tasks on your home dashboard, keeping your follow-up visible and accountable. When the deal closes, move it to Closed Won or Closed Lost — HubSpot will ask for a loss reason on Closed Lost, which builds useful data for retrospectives over time.

💡 David's tip: Skip the calendar-scheduling back-and-forth entirely by setting up HubSpot's free Meetings feature before you start prospecting. Go to Sales → Meetings, connect your Google or Outlook calendar, and create a meeting link. Share this link in emails, your email signature, and your website — prospects book time directly on your calendar without a single reply-chain. This is one of HubSpot's most underrated free features, and it's comparable to Calendly's paid tier. Most calendar apps charge $8–15/month for this functionality; in HubSpot Free it's included, one link per user.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is HubSpot CRM really free?

Yes. HubSpot CRM's core is permanently free with no time limit and no credit card required. The free plan includes unlimited contacts, unlimited users, a deal pipeline, email tracking, meeting scheduling, live chat, and basic reporting. Paid upgrades (Starter at $15/seat/mo, Professional at $90/seat/mo) unlock marketing automation, sequences, and advanced analytics — but many small businesses run on the free tier indefinitely.

How long does HubSpot CRM setup take?

Most small teams complete the five steps in this guide in under an hour. Account creation takes about 5 minutes. Pipeline customization takes 10–15 minutes if you have your sales stages defined. Contact import from a CSV typically takes 15–20 minutes including field mapping and deduplication review. Email connection takes under 5 minutes for Gmail or Outlook. You can be tracking your first deal within an hour of starting.

Can I use HubSpot CRM without a website?

Yes. HubSpot CRM does not require a website to function. The core CRM features — contacts, deals, pipeline, email, and calling — work independently of any website. The website and landing page tools are separate HubSpot products. You can manage your entire sales process inside the CRM without ever connecting it to a website.

What happens when I upgrade from HubSpot Free?

Upgrading from HubSpot Free to a paid plan adds features on top of your existing free setup — nothing is removed and your existing contacts, deals, and pipeline stages carry over. Starter ($15/seat/mo) adds email sequences, a calling tool with recorded calls, task queues, and removes HubSpot branding from emails. Professional ($90/seat/mo) unlocks sales automation, playbooks, forecasting, and custom reporting. You can also purchase individual Hubs (Marketing Hub, Sales Hub, Service Hub) rather than the full platform.
