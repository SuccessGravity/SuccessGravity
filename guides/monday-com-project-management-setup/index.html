📅 How to Set Up monday.com for Project Management (Step-by-Step 2026 Guide)

By David · Founder & Editor, Success Gravity · ✓ Updated November 2026

Disclosure: This guide may contain affiliate links. If you buy through them, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Learn more.

monday.com is one of the more polarizing project management tools: people who get it configured properly love it deeply, while teams that sign up, poke around for 20 minutes, and give up rarely discover what made them look in the first place. The gap between those two experiences almost always comes down to the board setup. A blank monday.com board is not very useful; a well-configured board with the right column types, group structure, and automations is one of the most visible and adaptable project tracking systems available today.

What makes monday.com different, in our view, is not any single feature — it is the degree to which you can make one board look like a task list, a Gantt chart, a CRM pipeline, or a resource allocation grid depending entirely on how you configure it. That flexibility is real, but it requires deliberate setup. This five-step guide covers everything you need to go from a fresh monday.com account to a working project management system: picking the right workspace structure, choosing columns that actually serve your workflow, inviting your team, building automations that cut manual work, and creating a dashboard that gives leadership visibility without anyone filing a status report.

monday.com setup flow: Account → Workspace → Board → Team → Dashboard. Each stage builds on the last.

Step 1: Create Your Account and Workspace

Start at monday.com and sign up with your work email. After account creation, monday.com immediately asks you to name your first workspace and choose a use case — marketing, software development, operations, HR, and several others. The use case selection loads a relevant template library into your sidebar; it does not lock you into anything. Pick the option closest to your primary use case to get useful starter boards in your template library.

Workspaces in monday.com function as containers that group related boards. A single-team company typically uses one workspace — your company name — containing all project boards. A larger organization might have separate workspaces for departments: Marketing Workspace, Engineering Workspace, Operations Workspace. Members can be given access to individual workspaces without seeing others, which is the main reason to create multiple workspaces rather than putting everything in one. For most small and mid-sized teams, a single workspace is the right starting point. You can add additional workspaces later from the left sidebar without disrupting existing boards.

During the sign-up flow, monday.com also prompts you to invite team members. Skip this for now — it is easier to invite people once you have at least one board configured, so they land in a workspace that is already meaningful rather than an empty default. The plan selection question matters: monday.com's pricing page shows that the free plan covers up to 2 seats and 3 boards, which is enough to build your initial structure and test the tool but not enough for a real team. The Standard plan at around $12 per user per month is where the most important features unlock — timeline view, automations, integrations, and guest access — and is the tier worth evaluating once your boards are set up.

Step 2: Set Up Your First Board and Choose Column Types

Boards are the central unit of work in monday.com. A board represents a project, a workflow, or any recurring process — a content calendar, a product launch tracker, a client onboarding pipeline. Create a new board by clicking + Add in the left sidebar, then choosing whether to start from a template or a blank board. For your first board, the blank option is worth trying — it forces you to decide which columns you actually need rather than inheriting a template's assumptions about your workflow.

Inside a board, each row is an item (a task, a deliverable, a contact, a deal — whatever your workflow calls it). Items are grouped into Groups, which function as phases, sprints, or categories. For a project board, typical group names might be: To Do, In Progress, In Review, Done. For a content calendar, they might be: Ideas, Writing, Scheduled, Published. Groups can be collapsed and are color-coded, making the board easy to scan at a glance.

Column types are where monday.com's flexibility really shows — and in our view, the column customization is what makes it distinctly different from competitors. The key column types to know:

  • Status — a colored label column with customizable options. This is the heartbeat of most boards and what automation rules react to. Set your Status options to match your actual workflow phases before inviting teammates.
  • Person — assigns items to workspace members. Supports multiple assignees. This is what automation uses to send notifications to the right person when status changes.
  • Date — a single deadline date. Pairs with the Timeline column for Gantt-style planning.
  • Timeline — a date range bar representing start and end dates. When you switch to Timeline view, these bars draw the Gantt chart automatically with no additional setup.
  • Numbers — a numeric field. Can be summed, averaged, or charted. Useful for story points, budget, hours estimated, or any quantity that needs aggregation.
  • Files — file attachment column. Keeps deliverables attached to the item they belong to rather than buried in a shared folder.
  • Dropdown — a single-select or multi-select text label. Useful for Priority (High / Medium / Low), Team (Marketing / Dev / Design), or any categorical tag that does not need a colored status.

Add columns by clicking the + at the right end of the column header row. Rename any column by clicking its name. The order and selection of columns should reflect the information your team needs to see at a glance — not every possible piece of data. A board with 12 columns is harder to scan than one with 6 well-chosen columns. Start with Status, Person, Date, and one or two custom columns specific to your project. Add more once you are using the board actively and discover what is missing.

Step 3: Invite Your Team and Assign Work

With at least one board configured, invite team members from Main Menu → Invite Members or directly from a board via the Invite button in the top right. monday.com's permission structure has three main levels: Admin (full account control, billing access), Member (can create and edit boards, items, and content), and Viewer (read-only access — suitable for stakeholders or clients who need visibility without edit rights). There is also a Guest role (available on Standard and above) for external collaborators who should only see specific boards they are explicitly invited to, not the full workspace.

Once teammates are in the workspace, assign items using the Person column. Click the Person cell on any item, search for a team member's name, and select them. Items can have multiple assignees — useful for collaborative deliverables. Assigned team members receive a notification about the assignment and the item appears in their personal My Work view, which aggregates everything assigned to them across all boards they can access. This cross-board My Work view is one of monday.com's most practical daily-use features and something new team members discover with visible relief once shown.

Use @mentions in item updates (the speech-bubble icon on any item row) to loop in colleagues on specific discussions. @mentioning someone who is not assigned to the item notifies them of the comment without changing the item's assignees. This is the right pattern for one-off questions or flagging something for someone's awareness without reshuffling ownership of the work. Updates on items create a full comment thread and activity log that serves as a lightweight paper trail of decisions and status changes — far more discoverable than the equivalent conversation buried in a Slack thread.

Step 4: Build Automations to Eliminate Manual Updates

Automations are available starting from the Standard plan, and they are, in our assessment, the single best reason to pay for the upgrade over the free or Basic plan. monday.com's Automation Center (accessible via the Automations button at the top of any board) uses a "When / Then" recipe format that is readable and configurable without any technical knowledge.

The automations worth setting up first on a project board:

  • When status changes to "Done" → Notify item creator: Tells whoever created a task that it is finished, eliminating the need for assignees to send a manual message when they complete work.
  • When date arrives → Send reminder to person: Sends the assignee a notification on the due date. Pairs with a "2 days before due date" reminder for advance notice on high-stakes deliverables.
  • When item is created → Assign to [person]: Useful for boards where all incoming work goes to one person for triage — support queues, content idea boards, bug trackers. New items are automatically routed without anyone manually checking for unassigned rows.
  • When status changes to "In Review" → Assign to [reviewer]: Passes work to the reviewer automatically when the author marks it ready, removing the hand-off message from the workflow entirely.

Automations on monday.com also support cross-board actions (Standard and above): creating an item in a different board, moving an item from one board to another, or mirroring a status change across connected boards. Cross-board automations are what enable more sophisticated workflows — for example, automatically creating a project board from a template when a deal closes in a CRM board, or escalating overdue items from a team board into an executive visibility board. These require a bit more planning but represent the ceiling of what the platform can do as an orchestration layer across your business operations.

💡 David's tip: Build your automations after your Status column is fully configured — not before. Automation rules reference specific Status label names (e.g., "Done" or "In Review"), and renaming a Status label after the automation is set up breaks the rule silently. Lock in your Status options first, then wire the automations. This two-step order saves a debugging session you would otherwise spend wondering why notifications stopped firing.

Step 5: Create a Dashboard for Project Visibility

Dashboards in monday.com are separate from boards — they are reporting layers that pull data from one or more boards and display it as widgets. Create a new Dashboard from the left sidebar by clicking + Add → Dashboard. A single dashboard can connect to multiple boards, which is what makes it useful for leadership-level visibility: you can see the status of every active project in one place without switching between individual boards.

The widgets most useful for project management dashboards:

  • Battery widget: Shows the percentage of items in each Status across a connected board. Gives an instant visual read on overall project health — green battery means most items are Done, red battery means they are stuck. We find this widget to be the single most effective "is this project on track?" indicator available.
  • Numbers widget: Aggregates a Numbers column value across items — total hours, total budget, sum of story points. Useful for capacity and budget tracking at a glance.
  • Chart widget: Bar, pie, or line charts of board data. The most common use is a bar chart of items by Status — a visual task distribution overview useful for stand-ups.
  • Calendar widget: Shows all due-date fields across connected boards in a calendar view. Useful for spotting deadline clusters — weeks where multiple projects have deliverables arriving simultaneously that might strain team capacity.
  • Workload widget: Visualizes how many items or hours are assigned to each person across connected boards. The most reliable way to identify when someone is overloaded before deadlines are missed rather than after.

Share a Dashboard with specific team members or embed it in a monday.com Doc for stakeholder updates. Setting up a Dashboard early — before the first sprint or project phase ends — means you have reporting available from day one rather than scrambling to build it after someone asks for a status overview. A well-built Dashboard removes the need for most recurring status meetings because the information is always visible and always current.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is monday.com free?

Yes. monday.com offers a free plan limited to 2 seats and up to 3 boards. It is functional for solo users or pairs but becomes limiting for most teams. The Standard plan at around $12 per user per month (billed annually) is where the most important features unlock — timeline view, automations, integrations, and guest access. According to monday.com's pricing page, annual billing provides a meaningful discount over monthly.

How is monday.com different from Trello or Asana?

monday.com is built around a highly visual board where every column type is customizable — Status, Person, Date, Numbers, Timeline, Dropdown, Files, and many more. Trello uses a fixed Kanban card model with limited column types. Asana uses a task-centric hierarchy with sections. monday.com's column system means the same board can serve as a project tracker, a CRM pipeline, or a resource allocation grid depending on how you configure it — this flexibility is monday.com's primary differentiator, though it requires deliberate setup to realize.

Can I use monday.com for both projects and CRM?

Yes. monday.com offers a dedicated monday CRM product and a Sales CRM template. These run on the same Work OS as project management but are configured with contact, deal, and pipeline-specific columns. You can manage projects and CRM in the same account — different boards in different folders — and use automations to link them, such as creating a project board automatically when a deal closes. The CRM functionality is a genuine secondary use case, not an afterthought.

How many automations do I get on the free plan?

The free plan includes no automations. Automations are available starting from the Standard plan, which includes 250 automation actions per month. The Pro plan increases this to 25,000 actions per month, and the Enterprise plan offers unlimited automations. If automation is central to how your team plans to use monday.com, the Standard plan at around $12 per user per month is the minimum viable tier.
