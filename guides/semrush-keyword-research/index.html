🔍 How to Use Semrush for Keyword Research: 5 Steps to Find Keywords That Actually Rank

By David · Founder & Editor, Success Gravity

Last updated: October 2026 · Pricing verified

Most people start by guessing: they write about topics that feel relevant, cross their fingers, and wait months to learn whether Google will put them on page one. The problem usually isn't the writing — it's the lack of a systematic way to find keywords you can realistically win. Keyword research sounds like a technical chore, but in practice it is the single most important strategic decision you make before you write a single word.

Semrush is built for exactly this. It's not the cheapest tool on the market, but it combines a massive keyword database, competitor gap analysis, and position tracking in one place. This guide walks through a five-step workflow — from setting up a project on day one to monitoring whether your targeted keywords are actually moving up. If you're tired of publishing content that disappears into page five, this is the process that fixes it.

Step 1: Set up your Semrush project and connect your domain

After signing into Semrush, navigate to Projects in the left sidebar and click Create project. Enter your domain exactly as it appears in your address bar — with or without "www" but not both — because Semrush will track data specifically for the version you enter, and mixing formats creates confusing splits in your reports. Give the project a descriptive name, confirm the target country for your primary audience, and save it. This project becomes the anchor for everything else in this guide: position tracking, site audits, and gap analysis all pull from the same project settings, so getting the domain right here saves you from correcting it downstream. Once the project is saved, Semrush will begin crawling your domain in the background — you'll see initial data populate within a few hours.

Step 2: Use the Keyword Magic Tool to find seed keywords

Open Keyword Research → Keyword Magic Tool and type a broad "seed" keyword that describes your main topic — something like "project management software," "small business loans," or "home gym equipment." Semrush will return thousands of related phrases grouped into topic clusters on the left sidebar. The clusters are the real value here: instead of scrolling thousands of rows, you click a cluster (say, "for beginners" or "free") and instantly narrow to semantically related keywords within that theme. The cluster view is a fast way to surface long-tail variations that are easy to miss when building keyword lists by hand. Start with your top three or four clusters and export those subsets before going broader; trying to work all 10,000 results at once leads to analysis paralysis and wasted time.

Step 3: Analyze keyword difficulty and search intent

With your cluster filtered down to a manageable list, add two columns to your view: KD% (Keyword Difficulty) and Intent. Keyword Difficulty runs from 0 to 100, and the score represents how strong the top-ranking pages are — a score of 70+ means you are competing against domain-authority powerhouses that have thousands of backlinks pointing at that exact page. Search Intent tells you what the person typing that query actually wants: Informational (they want to learn), Navigational (they want a specific site), Commercial (they are comparing options), or Transactional (they are ready to buy). A common mistake is matching keyword difficulty without matching intent — you can rank for an "informational" keyword with a blog post, but if the SERP is dominated by product pages, Google has decided the intent is commercial, and your article will struggle regardless of how well it is optimized. Filter for Intent first, then layer on KD to find genuinely attainable targets.

Step 4: Run a Keyword Gap analysis against competitors

Navigate to Competitive Research → Keyword Gap and enter your domain plus two or three direct competitors. Semrush will show you a Venn diagram of overlapping and exclusive keyword sets. The section you care about most is Missing — keywords your competitors rank for that your site does not appear for at all. This is not a list of keywords to copy; it is a list of topics your audience is clearly searching for that your content strategy has not addressed yet. Sort the Missing keywords by Volume descending, then apply the KD filter to keep only those under 50, and you have a prioritized hit list ready to hand to any writer.

Step 5: Set up Position Tracking to monitor your rankings

Publishing content targeting the right keywords is only half the job — you need to know whether it is actually working. Inside your project, open Position Tracking and add the keywords from your shortlist (Semrush lets you import directly from Keyword Magic). Set your target device (desktop and mobile track separately, and rankings differ more than most people expect), confirm your target location, and save. Within 24 hours you will have a baseline ranking for every keyword you added. From there, Semrush emails you a weekly summary of significant movements so you are not checking the dashboard obsessively. The real value shows up around weeks four to eight, when you can see whether a freshly published page is climbing from position 40 toward page one — or sitting stuck, which tells you the page needs stronger internal links, better on-page optimization, or a more targeted angle than your current draft provides.

💡 David's tip: In the Keyword Magic Tool, filter by KD under 40 before you look at anything else. I know it feels like you're leaving traffic on the table, but most new and mid-sized sites simply cannot compete for high-difficulty terms yet — not without a significant backlink campaign running in parallel. When you filter KD < 40 first, you are not settling for scraps; you are building a realistic content calendar full of terms you can actually win in the next 60–90 days. The volume on those keywords adds up faster than you think, and ranking positions one through three on a 1,200 monthly search volume keyword brings in more traffic than ranking positions eight through twelve on a 10,000 volume term.

Once you have rankings coming in, the next important move is connecting your Semrush data to what you are seeing in Google Analytics and Search Console. Rankings without conversion data tell you half the story; pairing position tracking with your GA4 key events shows you which keywords are not just bringing clicks but actually driving the business actions you care about — signups, purchases, contact form submissions. That combined view is where the real optimization decisions live.

The broader workflow here is intentionally sequential because each step narrows the universe. You start with thousands of potential keywords in the Keyword Magic Tool, cut them by difficulty and intent in step three, identify the highest-leverage gaps in step four, and then track only the refined shortlist in step five. Trying to track every keyword Semrush surfaces would create noise, not signal. Discipline in this filtering phase is what separates content strategies that compound over time from ones that produce a scattered collection of pages none of which rank well.

One more thing worth calling out: Semrush updates its keyword difficulty scores as the competitive landscape changes, so a keyword you checked six months ago and set aside because KD was 55 might now be at 42. It is worth revisiting your "too hard" list quarterly. Previously borderline keywords can become attainable when a dominant page drops in authority or when Semrush's underlying index refines its scoring. Building a habit of quarterly keyword audits is one of the highest-leverage maintenance tasks in an ongoing SEO program.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Semrush free for keyword research?

Semrush offers a limited free plan that lets you run up to 10 keyword searches per day and see partial results. For serious keyword research — especially gap analysis and position tracking — you need at least the Pro plan ($139/month, or $117.33/month billed annually). A free 7-day trial of the full Pro plan is available and worth using before committing.

What’s the difference between Keyword Difficulty and Search Volume?

Search Volume tells you how many people search for a term each month — it measures demand. Keyword Difficulty (KD) tells you how hard it will be to rank on page one — it measures competition. A high-volume, high-KD keyword is a traffic opportunity that's very hard to win. The real sweet spot is moderate volume (500–5,000/month) combined with a KD under 40, especially for newer or smaller sites.

How often should I check my keyword rankings?

Weekly is the right cadence for most sites. Daily checking creates anxiety without actionable signal — rankings fluctuate naturally by a few positions every day. Set Semrush's Position Tracking to send you a weekly email summary, and do a deeper manual review once a month to spot trends, flag pages that dropped more than five positions, and identify new ranking opportunities.

Can Semrush help with local SEO keyword research?

Yes. In the Keyword Magic Tool, you can filter results by country and by device type, and Semrush's database includes geo-modified keywords like "plumber in Austin" or "best pizza Chicago." For hyperlocal tracking, the Position Tracking tool lets you specify a zip code or city so you see rankings as Google serves them in that exact location — not just national averages.
