🕑 How to Use Buffer for Social Media Scheduling (Step-by-Step)

By David · Founder & Editor, Success Gravity · Updated October 2026

Buffer is one of the simplest tools available for scheduling social media posts across multiple platforms — and it has a free plan that covers the core workflow. This guide walks through the complete setup: connecting accounts, building a posting queue, setting a schedule, and using Buffer's analytics to make informed adjustments.

What you'll need

  • A Buffer account (free plan works for this guide)
  • Admin access to the social media accounts you want to connect
  • Chrome, Firefox, or Edge for the browser extension (optional but useful)

Step 1: Create Your Buffer Account and Choose a Plan

Go to buffer.com and create a free account using your email address or Google login. Buffer's free plan connects up to 3 social channels and holds up to 10 scheduled posts per channel at a time — enough to evaluate whether the tool fits your workflow before paying anything.

If you manage more channels or need analytics beyond the basic post performance view, Buffer's Essentials plan charges per channel (check buffer.com/pricing for current rates). For most individuals and small businesses starting out, the free plan is the right starting point: set it up, run it for a few weeks, and upgrade only if you hit the limits.

Step 2: Connect Your Social Media Channels

From your Buffer dashboard, click Connect Channels (or find the option in the left sidebar). Buffer will show a list of supported platforms including Instagram, Facebook Pages, X (Twitter), LinkedIn, Pinterest, TikTok, and others. Select the platforms you use.

Each connection goes through that platform's standard OAuth authorization flow — Buffer asks for the permissions it needs to publish on your behalf, and you approve. For Instagram, you'll need a Business or Creator account connected to a Facebook Page; personal Instagram accounts can't be scheduled for direct auto-publish. For Facebook Groups, you'll need to be an admin of the group. Buffer's help center at buffer.com/library documents the exact requirements per platform, and these sometimes change as platform APIs update.

Once connected, each channel appears in your Buffer left sidebar with its own queue.

Step 3: Set Your Posting Schedule

Buffer's scheduling works around a queue: you add posts and Buffer sends them out at the next available slot in your pre-set schedule. Setting up that schedule is the most important configuration step.

Click a channel in your sidebar, then go to Posting Schedule (or Settings → Schedule depending on your Buffer plan). You'll see a weekly calendar view where you can add time slots for each day. For example, you might set Mon/Wed/Fri at 9 AM and Tue/Thu at 12 PM — Buffer will fill those slots in order as you add posts to the queue.

A practical starting schedule for most channels is 3–5 posts per week at times aligned with your audience's active hours. Buffer's analytics (on paid plans) can show when your audience is most active. For a new account with no data, a common starting point is morning (8–10 AM) and early afternoon (12–2 PM) in your audience's primary timezone.

You can also set different schedules per channel — posting more frequently on X than on LinkedIn is a common setup, since content norms and audience expectations differ across platforms.

Step 4: Create Posts and Add Them to Your Queue

Click New Post (or the pencil/create button) in your dashboard. The composition window lets you write your post content, add images or video, and choose which channels to publish to. You can post the same content to multiple channels at once, or customize the text per channel — useful since hashtag usage, character limits, and tone vary by platform.

Buffer's post composer shows a character count per platform (LinkedIn, X, and Facebook each have different limits) and previews how your post will appear. Once you've written and previewed the post, choose from three options:

  • Add to Queue — places the post in the next available schedule slot
  • Schedule Post — lets you pick a specific date and time manually
  • Share Now — publishes immediately without waiting for a queue slot

For a consistent posting routine, Add to Queue is the most efficient option: you batch-write posts when you have time, and Buffer handles the timing without you having to think about it.

Step 5: Use the Buffer Browser Extension for Quick Sharing

Buffer's browser extension (available for Chrome, Firefox, and Edge via the extension stores) adds a Buffer button to your browser that lets you share any webpage, article, or image directly to your queue without opening the Buffer dashboard. This is one of Buffer's most useful features for content curation: when you're reading an article you want to share, click the extension, write your commentary, and add it to the queue in about 30 seconds.

On mobile, Buffer's iOS and Android apps let you share content to Buffer from any app via the native share sheet — useful for sharing Instagram content, screenshots, or links discovered in other apps.

Step 6: Review Analytics and Refine Your Schedule

Buffer's free plan shows basic post-level metrics: impressions, clicks, and engagement for each published post. Paid plans add channel-level analytics, audience growth tracking, and best-time-to-post recommendations.

Look at your analytics every two to four weeks with a simple question: which posts performed above average, and what do they have in common? Common patterns include specific post formats (images vs. text-only), topics, length, or posting times that outperform others. Use those patterns to adjust your queue strategy — write more of what works, less of what doesn't, and refine your posting schedule slots based on actual performance data.

For channels where you see strong engagement but limited reach, consider gradually increasing posting frequency — Buffer makes it easy to add more schedule slots per day. For channels with low engagement across all posts, the issue is usually content-related rather than timing: focus on post quality and relevance before adding volume.

Tips for Getting the Most Out of Buffer

  • Batch your writing. Set aside one session per week (30–60 minutes) to fill your queue for the coming week. This is more efficient than writing individual posts each day and produces more consistent output.
  • Use channel-specific customization. Even when posting the same core message across platforms, adjust copy for each: add relevant hashtags for Instagram, keep it concise for X, use a more professional tone for LinkedIn.
  • Audit your queue weekly. Before adding new posts, check your queue to make sure nothing is outdated, time-sensitive, or inconsistent with something that happened since you scheduled it.
  • Don't set-and-forget. Buffer handles scheduling, not community management. Respond to comments and engage with replies manually — post-level interactions are what drives algorithm reach on most platforms.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Buffer free?

Buffer has a free plan that allows you to connect up to 3 channels and schedule up to 10 posts per channel at a time. It's functional for individuals managing a small presence. Paid plans (Essentials, from $6/month per channel) remove the queue limit and add analytics and engagement features. Current pricing is on buffer.com.

Which social platforms does Buffer support?

Buffer supports Instagram, Facebook Pages and Groups, X (Twitter), LinkedIn Pages and Profiles, Pinterest, TikTok, YouTube, Google Business Profile, Mastodon, and Threads. Platform support changes as APIs update — check buffer.com/channels for the current list.

Can Buffer post to Instagram automatically?

Buffer can auto-publish single images, videos, and carousels to Instagram Business and Creator accounts. Instagram Stories can be scheduled but require a manual publish step on your phone, since Meta's API doesn't allow automated Story publishing. Check buffer.com/library for the latest on what each Instagram format supports.

What is the difference between Buffer and Hootsuite?

Buffer is simpler and cheaper — built primarily around a clean scheduling queue and basic analytics. Hootsuite is a fuller social media management platform with deeper social listening, team workflows, and CRM integrations, but costs significantly more. For individuals and small teams focused on scheduling, Buffer's free or low-cost paid plans typically cover everything needed.
