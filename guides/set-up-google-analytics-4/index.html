📊 How to Set Up Google Analytics 4 (Properly) in 15 Minutes

By David · Founder & Editor · Updated September 2026

Every week I meet business owners who spend real money on ads and content but cannot answer one question: did any of it work? Google Analytics 4 answers that question for free — but only if you set it up properly. Most people install the tag, see a visitors number, and stop. The visitors number is the least useful thing GA4 gives you.

This is the exact 15-minute setup I run on every new site I launch, including this one. The goal is not “having analytics” — it is knowing which pages and channels produce the actions you care about: purchases, signups, contact-form messages.

Step 1: Create your account and property

Go to analytics.google.com, sign in with your business Google account, and click Start measuring. Name the account after your business and the property after your website. Pick your time zone and currency carefully — every report you ever read will use them, and changing them later muddies historical comparisons.

Step 2: Add a web data stream

When prompted for a platform, choose Web and enter your domain. GA4 creates a data stream and shows your Measurement ID (it looks like G-XXXXXXX). Leave “Enhanced measurement” on — it automatically tracks scrolls, outbound clicks, site search, and file downloads with zero extra work.

Step 3: Install the Google tag

GA4 gives you a small code snippet (the gtag). If you use WordPress, Wix, Squarespace, or Shopify, you don't need to touch code — each has a settings field for the Measurement ID (look for “Google Analytics” in marketing or integration settings). On a custom site, paste the snippet into the <head> of every page. Verify with the Realtime report: open your site in another tab and you should see yourself as one active user within a minute.

Step 4: Mark your key events — the step everyone skips

Traffic tells you people came; key events tell you the visit mattered. In Admin → Events, find the events that represent success — form_submit for a contact form, purchase for a store, or a custom event for a newsletter signup — and flip the Mark as key event toggle. From then on, every traffic report can be sliced by “which source produced key events,” which is the only version of the report worth reading.

Step 5: Link Search Console and give it two weeks

In Admin → Product links → Search Console, link your Search Console property (set that up with our GSC guide if you haven't). Then resist the urge to check hourly. GA4 needs about two weeks of data before its reports mean anything — set a calendar reminder and come back to the Traffic acquisition report with fresh eyes.

💡 David's tip: Create key events the same week you install GA4. If you wait, you'll have months of traffic data with no way to know which traffic was worth anything.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Google Analytics 4 really free?

Yes — GA4 is free at any traffic volume a small business will ever see. Google 360, the paid enterprise tier, only becomes relevant at millions of events per month.

Do I need Google Tag Manager too?

Not on day one. GTM is worth adding when you start managing multiple pixels (Meta, TikTok, conversion tags) — it lets you add and edit them without touching site code. For GA4 alone, the direct tag is simpler.

Why does my visitor count differ from my hosting stats?

Hosting counts every request, including bots. GA4 filters most bots and measures people. When the two disagree, GA4 is closer to the truth for business decisions.

Is GA4 GDPR/privacy compliant?

GA4 removed IP storage and offers consent mode, but compliance depends on your setup — if you serve EU visitors, add a consent banner and enable consent mode. For US-only small business sites, a clear privacy policy mentioning analytics is the baseline.
