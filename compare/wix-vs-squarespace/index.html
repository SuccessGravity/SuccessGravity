Quick Summary

Wix wins on flexibility and feature breadth. Its drag-and-drop editor gives you precise control over every element, and its broader range of built-in apps makes it easier to build blended businesses. Squarespace wins on design quality and polish. Its curated templates and constrained editing model produce more visually consistent results, and it edges ahead on ecommerce transaction fees at the business tier. Both are solid choices for small businesses and personal sites — the decision mostly comes down to what you value more: total control or a beautifully managed outcome.

What Each Platform Is Built For

Wix was designed around one principle: maximum editorial freedom. Its drag-and-drop editor lets you place any element — text, images, video, buttons, forms — anywhere on the canvas, with pixel-level control over size, position, and spacing. That freedom makes Wix the most flexible all-in-one builder on the market for non-developers, and it's why the platform dominates among first-time site builders, small businesses, and anyone who wants a site that doesn't look like a template. Wix's breadth of built-in features — bookings, events, memberships, online stores, blogs, restaurants, music players — means it can accommodate a wide range of business models without third-party apps. The trade-off is that total freedom can produce inconsistent layouts if you're not deliberate about design discipline.

Squarespace was built around a different philosophy: curated quality over maximum control. Its templates are designed to a higher aesthetic standard than most competitors, and the editor deliberately constrains how much you can deviate from each template's layout — not to frustrate you, but to make it harder to accidentally produce a poorly designed page. The result is a platform where even beginners with no design training tend to produce professional-looking sites more reliably than on editors with no guardrails. Squarespace's sweet spot is creative professionals — photographers, designers, authors, musicians, and boutique product brands — who want a site that looks expensive without hiring a developer. The narrower template selection (~140 versus Wix's 900+) is a genuine limitation for users who want maximum variety, but the quality of what's there is consistently higher.

Templates and Design

Wix offers more than 900 templates spanning dozens of categories — from restaurant and portfolio to online store and events. Every template is fully editable: you can move any element anywhere, change colors and fonts globally or individually, and add, remove, or resize sections without restriction. This makes it possible to produce a site that looks completely different from the starting template, which is both its strength and a risk — the flexibility means design quality depends heavily on the decisions the person building the site makes.

Squarespace's approximately 140 premium templates are fewer in number but consistently executed at a higher visual standard. They are designed around a section-based editing model: you add, remove, and reorder full-width sections rather than positioning individual elements freely. This constrained system means Squarespace sites tend to have better visual rhythm and more consistent typography than the average Wix site built without design training. For users who want a polished result without investing heavily in design decisions, the constrained model is an advantage. For users who have a specific, unconventional layout in mind, it can feel limiting. Both platforms let you change colors, fonts, and content; only Wix lets you move individual elements to arbitrary positions.

Ecommerce Features

Both Wix and Squarespace support selling physical products, digital downloads, and services online. Squarespace charges a 0% transaction fee on its Business and Commerce plans — you pay only your payment processor's standard rate (typically around 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction through Stripe). Wix Payments similarly charges 0% platform transaction fees on its Business plans. Neither platform adds an extra fee on top of the payment processor, which is a meaningful advantage over platforms that charge 1–2% additional fees.

Squarespace's ecommerce feature set is strong for small and medium product catalogs — abandoned cart recovery, discount codes, product reviews, subscriptions, and a clean checkout experience are all included on the Commerce tiers. Wix's ecommerce capabilities are broader in some ways: it supports bookings, events, restaurants (with table management and online ordering), and memberships alongside physical product selling, making it more versatile for blended business models. For pure retail with a focus on aesthetics, Squarespace competes well. For businesses that sell across multiple formats (products + services + bookings), Wix handles the combination more naturally.

SEO Tools

Both platforms include the SEO fundamentals any small business site needs: customizable meta titles and descriptions, XML sitemaps submitted to search engines, canonical tags, 301 redirects, and the ability to add structured data. Neither requires any technical SEO knowledge to set up a well-optimized basic site.

Wix differentiates itself with its SEO Setup Checklist — a guided wizard that walks you through each optimization step for your site, from verifying ownership in Google Search Console to filling in meta descriptions for every page. For beginners who don't know what SEO tasks to prioritize, this wizard is genuinely useful. Squarespace counters with clean, consistent URL structures (no redundant parameters or session IDs in URLs) and built-in AMP (Accelerated Mobile Pages) support for blog posts, which can improve mobile search visibility for content-heavy sites. Both platforms are SEO-capable for the kinds of sites they're designed to host; neither offers the full SEO tool suite of a dedicated platform like WordPress with Yoast or RankMath.

AI Features

Both Wix and Squarespace have integrated AI-assisted site creation to reduce the time it takes to get a first draft online.

Wix ADI (Artificial Design Intelligence) has been part of the platform for several years and works by asking a series of questions about your business type, preferred style, and content needs, then generating a complete site layout with placeholder content you edit. It is a practical shortcut for users who find a blank canvas intimidating and want a starting point to work from rather than choosing a template manually.

Squarespace Blueprint AI generates a site layout from text prompts describing your business and design preferences. Users describe what kind of site they want — a portfolio for a freelance illustrator, an online store for handmade ceramics — and Blueprint produces a section layout and page structure to build from. Both tools are primarily aimed at getting users past the blank-page problem; neither generates final copy or images autonomously. Both are improving iteratively, and the quality of AI-generated starting points has increased substantially across both platforms through 2025 and 2026.

Who Should Choose Wix?

  • Beginners who want a free starting point. Wix's free plan lets you build and publish a site at no cost (with Wix ads and a subdomain) — useful for testing before committing to a paid plan.
  • Blended businesses. Yoga studios, salons, consultants, musicians, and any business that sells products alongside services, memberships, or bookings will find Wix handles the combination more naturally than Squarespace.
  • Users who want precise design control. If you have a specific layout in mind and want full freedom to execute it, Wix's pixel-level editor delivers that without requiring code.
  • Small local businesses with varied needs. Restaurants (with online ordering and reservations), event organizers, and fitness instructors benefit from Wix's broader built-in feature set.
  • Anyone who wants the widest template variety. With 900+ templates, you're far more likely to find one that closely matches your industry and aesthetic starting point.

Who Should Choose Squarespace?

  • Creatives and portfolio-builders. Photographers, graphic designers, illustrators, and other visual creatives consistently rate Squarespace's portfolio templates as the most professional available without custom development.
  • Small product brands where aesthetics matter. Boutique clothing brands, artisan food producers, jewelry designers, and similar businesses benefit from Squarespace's design-forward templates and cohesive storefront presentation.
  • Writers and content publishers. Squarespace's unified blog and website management, combined with clean typography defaults, makes it a strong choice for authors, journalists, and content-led businesses.
  • Users who want great design with minimal effort. The constrained editing model means Squarespace produces more consistently polished results even for users with no design background.
  • Small ecommerce stores where price matters. Squarespace's Business plan at $23/month (with 0% transaction fees) offers strong value for stores selling fewer than a few hundred products.

The Verdict

Both platforms are strong choices for the users they're designed to serve. For creatives, independent professionals, and design-conscious small brands — photographers, designers, boutique retailers — Squarespace is the better pick. Its template quality is genuinely higher, its ecommerce pricing is more competitive at the entry level, and its constrained editing model reliably produces better-looking sites for users without design training. It earns a rating of 4.7/5.

For businesses that value flexibility, need to handle multiple revenue streams (products, bookings, events, memberships) in a single platform, or simply want a free plan to get started, Wix is the stronger choice. Its 900+ templates, powerful drag-and-drop editor, and broader built-in feature set make it the more versatile tool for diverse business types. It earns a rating of 4.6/5 — a close runner-up. The most straightforward advice: take advantage of both free options — Wix's permanent free plan and Squarespace's 14-day trial — and build a test page on each before committing. The platform that feels right in the first hour is usually the right long-term choice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Wix or Squarespace better for SEO?

Both platforms support the essential SEO basics — meta titles, meta descriptions, XML sitemaps, and canonical tags. Wix includes an SEO Setup Checklist wizard that walks users through on-page optimization steps and connects to Google Search Console. Squarespace is noted by SEO practitioners for its clean, consistent URL structures and built-in AMP support for blog content. Neither platform has a decisive edge for most small businesses, but both are fully capable of supporting a well-optimized site in the hands of a user who follows through on the basics.

Which is cheaper, Wix or Squarespace?

Squarespace's entry paid plan (Personal) starts at $16/month billed annually, making it marginally cheaper than Wix's Light plan at $17/month. For ecommerce specifically, Squarespace's Business plan at $23/month is notably cheaper than Wix's Core plan at $29/month for similar selling features. The overall price gap is small; both platforms sit in a similar range. Always verify current pricing on each vendor's pricing page before purchasing, as prices change.

Can I switch from Wix to Squarespace?

There is no direct migration tool between Wix and Squarespace. Switching platforms requires manually rebuilding your site on Squarespace — recreating pages, uploading images, re-entering products, and reconfiguring settings. Blog posts can sometimes be moved in limited formats via export/import, but the process involves meaningful manual effort regardless of site size. It's worth thoroughly evaluating both platforms on their free options before committing to avoid needing to switch later.

Which is better for ecommerce, Wix or Squarespace?

Both platforms charge 0% platform transaction fees — you pay only the payment processor's standard rate. Squarespace's ecommerce plans are priced more competitively at the entry level (Business at $23/month vs. Wix's Core at $29/month). Wix is the stronger choice for blended business models that combine products with bookings, events, or memberships. For pure product-based retail stores with a premium aesthetic focus, Squarespace competes strongly. Both platforms have a lower ceiling than dedicated ecommerce tools like Shopify or BigCommerce for large or complex catalogs.
