Quick Verdict

Choose Shopify if selling online is your primary goal. Its checkout, inventory management, and app ecosystem are built for commerce at every scale — from a first product to a multi-million dollar operation. Choose Wix if you want a website first and an online store second. Wix's true drag-and-drop editor, 900+ templates, and flexibility make it the better fit for service businesses, portfolios, restaurants, and local businesses that also want to sell a handful of products without committing to an ecommerce-first platform.

Shopify and Wix are both excellent platforms for getting a business online in 2026, but they were designed with fundamentally different goals. Shopify was built from day one around the question: how do you help a business sell more products online? Every feature — the checkout, inventory, shipping, apps, analytics — has been refined with that singular focus for nearly 20 years. Wix started from the opposite direction: how do you give anyone the ability to build a beautiful website without code? Commerce came later, and it shows in both the product's strengths and its limits. Here's how the two stack up when the marketing material is stripped away.

Ecommerce Features

This is Shopify's strongest suit, and the gap over Wix is meaningful at any serious scale. Shopify's checkout is one of the highest-converting in the industry — Shopify reports that its accelerated checkout using Shop Pay converts 36% higher than guest checkout on average. Its inventory management handles variants, bundles, and multi-location stock. Shipping integrations pull real-time rates from UPS, FedEx, DHL, and USPS. The 8,000+ apps in the Shopify App Store cover virtually every ecommerce use case: subscriptions, upsells, loyalty programs, B2B pricing, wholesale portals, dropshipping, print-on-demand, and advanced analytics.

Wix ecommerce — available on the Business plan (~$36/mo) and above — handles the basics well: products, variants, inventory, coupons, abandoned cart emails, subscriptions, and dropshipping through Modalyst. It supports multichannel selling on Facebook, Instagram, and Amazon. For a small shop selling up to a few hundred products, Wix ecommerce is entirely capable. What it lacks relative to Shopify is depth in inventory management (no multi-location stock natively), a smaller pool of ecommerce-specific apps (~900 total vs. Shopify's 8,000+), and less powerful shipping automation.

Winner: Shopify. Not even close if selling products online is the core business. The gap grows wider as order volume increases.

Design Flexibility

This is where Wix wins outright. Wix's editor is a genuine free-form drag-and-drop canvas: any element can be placed at any position on the page, pixel-precise, with no grid constraints. With over 900 professionally designed templates covering dozens of industries — restaurants, photographers, fitness studios, law firms, boutique hotels, and beyond — finding a starting point that suits a specific business type is easy. Wix also offers Wix AI, which can generate a complete website from a brief description in minutes, and its Editor X (Wix Studio) gives designers CSS-level control for complex layouts.

Shopify's design approach is more constrained by design. Themes define the structure of pages, and while you can customize within a theme — colors, fonts, section layouts, content blocks — truly freeform pixel placement isn't possible without code. Shopify has a solid selection of free themes (around 12) and a paid theme marketplace (from $170–$450 for premium themes). Its themes are optimized for ecommerce conversion, which is a real advantage, but they're not as visually diverse as Wix's template library. One Shopify note worth knowing: unlike Wix, switching to a different theme after launch is supported — your content migrates, though layout adjustments are needed.

Winner: Wix. For non-ecommerce businesses where the visual experience of the site matters as much as its function, Wix's design freedom is a genuine differentiator.

Pricing

Shopify and Wix are closer in price than many assume — but the comparison requires care because their plan structures serve different needs.

Shopify's plans (billed annually, approximate):

  • Basic: ~$29/mo — Full ecommerce, 2 staff accounts, shipping discounts up to 77%
  • Shopify: ~$79/mo — 5 staff accounts, better shipping discounts, professional reporting
  • Advanced: ~$299/mo — 15 staff accounts, custom reports, third-party calculated shipping rates
  • Shopify Plus: From ~$2,500/mo — Enterprise-grade, custom checkout, B2B features

Transaction fees: 0% when using Shopify Payments; 0.5–2% with third-party gateways depending on plan.

Wix's plans (billed annually, approximate):

  • Light: ~$17/mo — Website only, no ecommerce, custom domain
  • Core: ~$29/mo — Basic ecommerce, 5 collaborators
  • Business: ~$36/mo — Full ecommerce, subscriptions, abandoned cart recovery
  • Business Elite: ~$159/mo — Unlimited staff, custom reports, priority support

Pricing changes regularly — verify current plans on shopify.com and wix.com before committing. Annual billing unlocks the best rates.

Winner: Wix — slightly lower entry price for ecommerce, and the Light plan is a genuinely useful option for non-ecommerce websites that Shopify doesn't offer.

Ease of Use

Both platforms are accessible to non-technical users, but they're accessible in different ways. Wix's editor is immediately intuitive for anyone who has used drag-and-drop software — clicking anything puts it in edit mode, and the interface rewards visual exploration. The tradeoff is that Wix's design freedom can lead to inconsistent layouts if you're not deliberate about spacing and alignment, and its mobile editor requires a separate round of adjustments since the mobile view doesn't auto-adapt perfectly from the desktop layout.

Shopify's admin is clean and structured, particularly around store management: products, orders, customers, and analytics are well-organized. Setting up a Shopify store from scratch — adding products, configuring shipping, connecting a payment method — follows a logical wizard-driven flow. The complexity grows when customizing themes without code or configuring multi-currency and multi-market settings, where Shopify's depth becomes more apparent. The Shopify Help Center and an active community make most questions answerable without developer support.

Winner: Tie. Wix is more immediately intuitive for the design side; Shopify is more logical for the commerce setup side. Both rate highly for ease of use relative to self-hosted alternatives like WordPress + WooCommerce.

SEO Capabilities

Both platforms have matured their SEO capabilities meaningfully. Shopify generates clean, crawlable product pages with structured data (schema markup) for products, reviews, and breadcrumbs out of the box. It includes automatic XML sitemaps, mobile-optimized themes, and fast CDN delivery — all important technical SEO signals. The main Shopify SEO limitation is the fixed URL structure (products are always at /products/product-name) and lack of full redirect management on lower plans.

Wix has significantly improved its technical SEO since its early days. It now offers full meta tag control, custom URL slugs, canonical tags, structured data support, robot.txt editing, and automatic sitemaps. The Wix SEO Wiz walks users through on-page optimization steps. Advanced users can access the full SEO Panel for detailed control. For most businesses, Wix's SEO is now competitive with Shopify's on a per-page basis. Where Shopify has an edge is for large product catalogs, where its structured data for ecommerce schemas and faceted navigation handling are more robust.

Winner: Shopify for large ecommerce catalogs; Tie for most other business types.

App Ecosystems

Shopify's App Store is one of the most extensive in the website platform world: over 8,000 apps covering ecommerce, marketing, fulfillment, analytics, customer service, B2B, subscriptions, and more. For virtually any ecommerce need that isn't natively in Shopify — a specific loyalty program, a B2B pricing tier, a print-on-demand integration, an advanced review system — there's almost certainly a well-reviewed app for it. The tradeoff: app costs add up. It's not unusual for a growing Shopify store to pay $200–$600/month in third-party app subscriptions on top of the platform fee.

Wix's app market has around 900 apps covering marketing, analytics, communication, booking, social feeds, and ecommerce add-ons. It's more than enough for most small-to-medium businesses. First-party Wix solutions (Wix Bookings, Wix Events, Wix Restaurants, Wix Payments) are well-integrated and cover many common business workflows without needing third-party apps. The app market is notably thinner for specialized ecommerce needs, particularly around advanced inventory, wholesale, or fulfillment workflows.

Winner: Shopify. The sheer depth of the App Store is a material competitive advantage for businesses with specific ecommerce requirements.

Which Should You Choose?

Choose Shopify if…

  • Selling products online is your primary revenue source
  • You need advanced inventory, shipping, or B2B features
  • You'll scale to hundreds or thousands of SKUs
  • You want access to 8,000+ ecommerce-specific apps
  • Checkout conversion rates and payment processing matter

Choose Wix if…

  • You need a visually flexible website and ecommerce is secondary
  • You run a service business, restaurant, portfolio, or local business
  • You want true drag-and-drop design without code
  • You're on a tighter budget for a non-ecommerce site
  • You want Wix's built-in tools (Bookings, Events, Restaurants)

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I switch from Wix to Shopify later?

Yes, but it requires work. Wix allows you to export product data as a CSV, which Shopify can import. However, your page designs, blog content, and URL structure won't transfer automatically — expect to rebuild those. If ecommerce is growing quickly, migrating sooner rather than later reduces the technical debt. Shopify's migration guides and third-party migration services (Litextension, Cart2Cart) can automate much of the product data transfer.

Is Wix good for ecommerce?

Wix ecommerce works well for small shops selling up to a few hundred products — the Business plan includes abandoned cart recovery, subscriptions, dropshipping via Modalyst, and multichannel selling on Instagram and Facebook. Where it shows its limits is at scale: inventory management, shipping rules, B2B features, and the app ecosystem are all meaningfully less powerful than Shopify's. For a side hustle or service business selling a small catalog, Wix ecommerce is entirely adequate. For a business where ecommerce is the primary revenue driver, Shopify's depth matters.

Does Shopify have transaction fees?

Shopify charges additional transaction fees (0.5–2% depending on plan) if you use a third-party payment gateway instead of Shopify Payments. Using Shopify Payments — available in most countries — eliminates those fees. Wix charges no additional transaction fees beyond those imposed by its payment processors (Wix Payments, PayPal, Stripe). If you're outside a Shopify Payments territory, factor the transaction fee into your Shopify total cost.

Which is better for SEO: Shopify or Wix?

Both platforms have improved their SEO capabilities significantly by 2026. Shopify offers clean URL structures, automatic sitemaps, schema markup for products, and fast CDN delivery — it's well-suited for large product catalogs where structured data matters. Wix's SEO Assistant walks beginners through optimizations step by step, and its technical SEO has matured considerably. For pure SEO, the difference is small; content quality and backlinks matter far more than platform choice for most sites.

Final Verdict

We rate Shopify 4.8/5 and Wix 4.4/5 — a gap that reflects Shopify's deeper ecommerce infrastructure rather than any overall quality difference. Wix is the right answer for the majority of small businesses that want a website with some selling capability; Shopify is the right answer for businesses whose core operation is an online store. If you're currently a Wix user growing your ecommerce revenue past a few hundred orders per month, that growth itself is the signal to evaluate Shopify.
