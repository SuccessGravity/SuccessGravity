The Short Answer

ActiveCampaign wins on automation depth, CRM capability, and the ability to build genuinely complex multi-channel sequences. Mailchimp wins on ease of use, entry pricing, and a free plan that covers a lot of ground for smaller lists. The decision mostly comes down to one question: are you building a marketing system, or sending campaigns? If you're building sequences with branching logic, lead scoring, and CRM integration — ActiveCampaign. If you're sending newsletters and basic automation to a growing list — Mailchimp gets you there faster and for less money at early stages.

Automation & Sequences

This is the category where the gap is widest. ActiveCampaign's visual automation builder supports 135+ triggers, goal nodes, conditional splits, wait steps tied to real-time behavior, and pre-built automation "recipes" covering nearly every common email sequence pattern. The branching logic is genuinely expressive: you can build sequences that respond to website visits, purchase history, CRM field changes, email engagement scores, and custom event data simultaneously.

Mailchimp's Customer Journey builder has improved significantly in recent releases and now covers the most common automation use cases: welcome sequences, abandoned cart flows, re-engagement campaigns, and date-based triggers. Where the two diverge is conditional branching: ActiveCampaign's builder supports considerably more complex branching logic than Mailchimp's.

Winner: ActiveCampaign — by a wide margin for complex sequences; Mailchimp is competitive for standard use cases.

CRM & Contact Management

ActiveCampaign includes a built-in CRM with deal pipelines, contact scoring, task management, and full visibility into every email interaction, form submission, and website visit a contact has made — all in a single record. For businesses where sales and marketing need to share a view of the prospect journey, this is the feature that justifies the price premium over Mailchimp.

Mailchimp does not include a real CRM. It has contact profiles and some basic tagging, but no deal tracking, lead scoring, or pipeline view. Mailchimp is designed around contacts as email recipients rather than as sales prospects with a lifecycle. If your business uses a separate CRM (Salesforce, HubSpot, Pipedrive), Mailchimp integrates with most of them adequately — but you're maintaining two systems rather than one.

Winner: ActiveCampaign — the built-in CRM is the clearest differentiator for sales-driven businesses.

Ease of Use

Mailchimp is the more intuitive platform for most people. The email builder is drag-and-drop with a minimal learning curve, the dashboard is well-organized, and the template library is large enough to get a professional-looking campaign live in 30 minutes without prior experience. Mailchimp has been designed from the ground up for non-technical users, and it shows.

ActiveCampaign's breadth comes with a steeper onboarding curve. The automation builder takes a few hours to understand properly, the contact database has more fields and views than many users need, and the navigation rewards a mental model of how campaigns, automations, and CRM interact. Expect to invest some setup and learning time before it feels comfortable — the initial friction is real.

Winner: Mailchimp — easier to learn and faster to deploy a first campaign.

Email Templates & Design

Mailchimp's template library is larger and more polished out of the box — with categorized templates for newsletters, announcements, events, e-commerce, and seasonal campaigns. The drag-and-drop builder is among the most intuitive in the category. For non-designers who want to send a good-looking email quickly, Mailchimp has the more forgiving design experience.

ActiveCampaign's template selection is narrower but still professionally designed, and the email builder has improved substantially in recent releases. It supports drag-and-drop, conditional content blocks (show different content to different segments in the same send), and dynamic personalization tokens. For users who have moved past basic templates and want content that adapts based on contact data, ActiveCampaign's conditional blocks are a meaningful capability Mailchimp doesn't match cleanly.

Winner: Mailchimp — better template variety and easier design experience at entry level.

Deliverability

Both platforms have strong deliverability track records. Mailchimp enforces strict list hygiene standards — high bounce rates and spam complaints trigger account warnings quickly — which keeps their shared sending infrastructure clean and benefits all users. The strictness can feel frustrating if you're managing a list that's gone stale, but it's ultimately in your interest.

ActiveCampaign gives more flexibility in managing your sending configuration, including dedicated IP options on higher-tier plans. This matters for high-volume senders (hundreds of thousands of monthly sends) who want independent sending reputation rather than shared infrastructure. For most small and mid-size businesses, the deliverability difference is marginal — list hygiene and engagement rates are the bigger variables.

Winner: Tie — both are strong; Mailchimp's enforcement keeps shared pools cleaner, ActiveCampaign offers more control at scale.

Integrations

Both platforms have extensive integration libraries. ActiveCampaign connects with 900+ apps via native integrations and Zapier — including Shopify, WooCommerce, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Calendly, and most major CMS and e-commerce platforms. Its deep CRM integration means contact data flows bidirectionally between your other tools and the marketing platform.

Mailchimp also integrates broadly — 300+ native integrations plus Zapier — with particular strength in its Shopify and WooCommerce e-commerce integrations, which sync product data, purchase history, and revenue attribution for email segmentation. For e-commerce email marketing specifically, Mailchimp's native store data integrations are among the cleanest in the category.

Winner: ActiveCampaign — more integrations overall and stronger bidirectional data sync with the CRM.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ActiveCampaign better than Mailchimp?

For complex automation, multi-channel sequences, and built-in CRM, yes — ActiveCampaign is the stronger platform. For simplicity, getting started quickly, and budget-conscious early-stage email marketing, Mailchimp is the better fit. The answer depends on your workflow and business stage, not a single universal ranking.

Can I migrate from Mailchimp to ActiveCampaign?

Yes. ActiveCampaign offers a free migration service that handles contact import, list setup, and basic automation rebuilding. The import of contacts and segments is smooth; rebuilding Mailchimp automation flows in ActiveCampaign's visual builder takes more time but is well-documented. If automation depth is what's driving the switch, expect a few hours of setup work to recreate your existing sequences in the new environment.

Which has better deliverability — ActiveCampaign or Mailchimp?

Both are strong. Mailchimp's stricter enforcement of bounce and complaint thresholds keeps its shared sending pools clean, which benefits deliverability for all users. ActiveCampaign offers dedicated IP options on higher-tier plans for businesses with high sending volumes who want independent reputation. For most small-to-mid businesses with a clean list, the difference is marginal — engagement rates and list hygiene matter more than which platform you're on.

Does Mailchimp have a free plan in 2026?

Yes. Mailchimp's free plan covers up to 500 contacts and 1,000 email sends per month in 2026, with access to basic automations, a landing page builder, and the email template library. It's a genuinely usable free tier for early-stage businesses. ActiveCampaign has no free plan — it starts at ~$15/month (billed annually) after a 14-day free trial.
