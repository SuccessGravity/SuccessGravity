What Is Grammarly?

Grammarly is an AI writing assistant that checks and improves your writing as you type — catching grammar, spelling, and punctuation mistakes in real time, then going further with clarity rewrites, tone adjustments, and full generative AI drafting. Launched in 2009 as a grammar checker, it has evolved into a full writing layer that sits on top of nearly everything you already use.

That "everywhere" quality is the core of its appeal. Instead of pasting text into a separate editor, Grammarly follows you: a browser extension, desktop apps for Windows and Mac, a mobile keyboard, and native support inside Google Docs, Microsoft Word, Gmail, Outlook, Slack, and most web apps. Whatever you're typing, the same assistant is quietly checking it — which is exactly how a writing tool should work in practice.

Key Features

Real-time grammar, spelling & punctuation

The foundation is still the best-in-class checker. Grammarly underlines issues as you type and explains why something is wrong, not just what to change — subject-verb agreement, comma splices, misused homophones, inconsistent tense. In our testing it catches meaningfully more real errors than built-in spellcheckers in Word or Docs, with fewer false alarms than most competitors.

Clarity & tone rewrites

This is where Premium starts earning its money. Grammarly flags wordy or convoluted sentences and offers one-click rewrites that are usually tighter and easier to read. The tone detector tells you how a message is likely to land — confident, friendly, formal, or accidentally curt — which is surprisingly useful before you hit send on a delicate email.

Generative AI drafting & replies

Grammarly's generative AI can draft emails, replies, outlines, and short pieces from a prompt, or transform existing text (shorten it, make it more persuasive, change the tone). For everyday business communication it's fast and genuinely helpful. For long-form marketing content, it's serviceable but not the strongest tool available — more on that in the alternatives section.

Works everywhere you write

The browser extension, desktop apps, and mobile keyboard mean Grammarly covers essentially every text field you'll encounter in a day. There's no "open the app, paste your text" friction, and corrections behave consistently whether you're in a CRM, a CMS, or a chat window. This ubiquity is Grammarly's biggest structural advantage over rivals.

Plagiarism checker, style guides & team features

Premium and Pro plans include a plagiarism checker that compares your text against billions of web pages — valuable for students, editors, and anyone publishing client work. Business and Enterprise tiers add shared style guides, brand tones that keep a whole team's writing consistent, snippets, and analytics dashboards so managers can see adoption and impact across the organization.

Who Should Use Grammarly?

  • Professionals who live in email and Slack — the tone detector and clarity rewrites alone can save you from awkward or muddled messages every day.
  • Non-native English writers who want explanations, not just corrections, so their writing improves over time.
  • Students and academics who need clean prose plus a plagiarism check before submission.
  • Teams and support orgs that want every customer-facing message on-brand, with style guides enforcing consistency automatically.

If your main goal is generating blog posts and ad copy at volume rather than polishing what you write, Grammarly is the wrong primary tool — pair it with a content generator instead.

Top Alternatives

  • ProWritingAid — better for authors and long-form editors who want deeper style reports (pacing, readability, overused words) and has historically offered one-time license options instead of a subscription.
  • QuillBot — the budget pick if paraphrasing and summarizing are your priority; its grammar checking is decent but less polished than Grammarly's.
  • Jasper AI — better if you need to generate marketing content at scale rather than polish it. Many teams run Jasper for drafting and Grammarly for the final pass.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Grammarly free?

Yes. The free plan includes core grammar, spelling, and punctuation corrections plus a limited number of generative AI prompts, with no credit card required. Premium adds clarity rewrites, full-sentence suggestions, the plagiarism checker, and expanded AI use.

Is Grammarly Premium worth it?

If you write professionally every day — client emails, reports, published content — the clarity rewrites and plagiarism checker usually justify roughly $12/mo billed annually. If you mostly write casual messages, the free plan covers you. Start free and upgrade only when you keep hitting its limits.

Can I trust Grammarly's suggestions?

Mostly, but not blindly. Grammarly is occasionally wrong or over-cautious, especially with intentional style choices, technical jargon, and creative phrasing. Treat it as a sharp-eyed editor whose suggestions you review — never auto-accept everything.

Grammarly vs ProWritingAid — which is better?

Grammarly wins on real-time accuracy, polish, and working everywhere you type. ProWritingAid wins on depth of style analysis for long manuscripts and value for money. Everyday business writers should pick Grammarly; novelists and heavy self-editors should try ProWritingAid.

Final Verdict

Grammarly earns its rating of 4.6/5. In a market flooded with AI writing tools, it wins by being invisible and everywhere: it makes the writing you're already doing measurably better without changing how you work. The free plan is generous enough to prove the value, and Premium is an easy call for anyone whose credibility rides on their writing. Just keep your editorial judgment switched on — and if long-form content generation is the real job, pair it with a dedicated tool like Jasper.
