What Is Browse AI?

Browse AI is a no-code web data extraction platform that lets you train an AI "robot" to scrape any public webpage — without writing a single line of code. The core idea is that you visit the page you want to scrape, describe in plain English what data you want to extract (product names, prices, phone numbers, email addresses, listing details), and Browse AI builds a robot that can repeat that extraction on a schedule or on demand.

The platform sits in the growing category of no-code data tools aimed at the non-developer user — people who need structured web data to inform pricing decisions, fill a CRM, monitor a competitor, or build a market research dataset, but who don't have the time or technical background to maintain a Python script or manage a headless browser setup. Browse AI abstracts away the complexity of session handling, pagination, JavaScript rendering, and selector maintenance that typically accompanies web scraping projects.

The product is accessed entirely through the browser. After creating a robot, you can run it manually, set it to run on a recurring schedule (as frequently as every 5 minutes on paid plans), and configure alerts that fire when the extracted data changes — for example, when a competitor changes a price, a job listing appears, or a product comes back into stock. Extracted data can be viewed in Browse AI's dashboard, exported as CSV or JSON, or pushed automatically to connected apps via Zapier, Make, or the platform's REST API.

Browse AI also maintains a library of 250+ prebuilt robots covering popular sources — Amazon, LinkedIn, Google Maps, Zillow, Indeed, Yelp, and many more — so for common use cases, there is often no setup required at all beyond entering a URL and clicking run.

Key Features

No-code robot builder with plain-English setup

The centerpiece of Browse AI is its robot builder, which uses a browser extension to record your interaction with a webpage and label the elements you want to extract. You click on a price, highlight a list of company names, or point to a table of job postings, and Browse AI learns the pattern. For many sites, the robot is ready to run in under five minutes. More complex workflows — multi-step navigations, login-gated pages, pagination through dozens of result pages, or input fields that need values before extraction can begin — are also supported, though they require more configuration time. The interface is designed around guided prompts and visual labeling rather than CSS selectors or XPath, making it practical for users who have never worked with a developer tool.

250+ prebuilt robots

Browse AI's prebuilt robot library covers a wide range of popular data sources and use cases. Robots for extracting Amazon product listings, Google Maps business profiles, LinkedIn company pages, Glassdoor reviews, Indeed job postings, Zillow property data, and Yelp reviews are available without any custom setup — you enter a search term or URL, and the robot handles the rest. For teams whose scraping needs map to well-known sites, this library dramatically reduces time-to-value. Prebuilt robots are maintained by Browse AI's team and updated when target site structures change, which removes the maintenance burden that often makes self-built scrapers fragile over time.

AI change detection and monitoring

One of Browse AI's more distinctive features is its change-detection system. Rather than running a robot only when triggered manually, you can configure a monitoring robot that runs on a schedule — every 5 minutes, hourly, daily, or at a custom interval on paid plans — and sends an alert when the extracted data differs from the previous run. This is particularly useful for competitive price monitoring (alert when a competitor drops below a target price), inventory tracking (alert when an out-of-stock product becomes available), and compliance monitoring (alert when legal or policy text changes on a vendor's site). The alert system integrates with email, Slack, and connected apps, so notifications reach teams in the workflow tools they already use.

7,000+ integrations via Zapier, Make, and API

Browse AI connects to the broader automation ecosystem through native Zapier and Make integrations, which together cover over 7,000 apps. Common destinations for extracted data include Google Sheets (for live-updating spreadsheets), Airtable (for structured databases), HubSpot and Salesforce (for CRM enrichment), Slack and Notion (for team notifications and knowledge bases), and any custom endpoint via webhook. The REST API, available on all paid plans, allows developers to trigger robots, retrieve results, and manage robots programmatically, enabling Browse AI to function as a data layer inside larger internal tools or automated workflows.

Multi-step robots and bulk extraction

Beyond single-page extraction, Browse AI supports multi-step robots that navigate through paginated result lists, follow links to detail pages, and aggregate data from multiple sources in a single run. This covers use cases like extracting all listings from a directory and then visiting each listing's page to collect additional fields — a workflow that would otherwise require a custom scraping script. Bulk extraction mode lets you supply a list of URLs (via upload or a connected spreadsheet) and run the same robot across all of them in parallel, which suits tasks like enriching a lead list with data from each company's website or pulling pricing data from a set of competitor product pages.

Who Should Use Browse AI?

Browse AI's value proposition is strongest for a specific profile of user: someone who needs structured web data regularly, doesn't want to maintain a technical scraping setup, and whose extraction volumes fit comfortably within the Personal or Professional credit limits.

  • Sales and marketing teams generating leads from public directories, business listings, or event attendee pages. Extracting names, company names, titles, phone numbers, and LinkedIn URLs from a structured source like Google Maps or a conference exhibitor list can populate a CRM in minutes rather than hours of manual research.
  • E-commerce operators monitoring competitor pricing. Setting a Browse AI robot to check competitor product pages at regular intervals and alert on price changes provides the kind of market intelligence that enterprise pricing teams pay much more to acquire through dedicated repricing tools.
  • Market researchers and analysts building datasets from public sources — job postings, real estate listings, news headlines, product reviews — who need structured output rather than raw page dumps. The CSV and JSON export formats slot directly into spreadsheet and analysis workflows.
  • Small businesses and solo operators who want to automate data-collection tasks that currently eat into their time. Monitoring a supplier's site for stock updates, tracking a competitor's job listings (an indicator of growth areas), or aggregating customer reviews from multiple platforms are all tasks Browse AI handles without technical staff.
  • Operations and procurement teams that need to track supplier pricing or terms across multiple vendor sites without a dedicated data engineering function.

Where Browse AI is a less natural fit: developers who want full programmatic control, the ability to handle complex authentication flows, or the capacity to run millions of requests per day will outgrow Browse AI's abstractions and find tools like Apify or a self-hosted Playwright/Puppeteer setup more appropriate. Similarly, anyone whose scraping targets are predominantly JavaScript-heavy SPAs with sophisticated bot detection may find that Browse AI's success rate varies significantly by target site.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the limits of Browse AI's free plan?

Browse AI's free plan provides 50 credits per month. Each credit covers the extraction of up to 10 rows of data or one screenshot capture — roughly 500 rows of data monthly. That is enough to test a single scraping workflow or run small, occasional extractions, but insufficient for ongoing monitoring or any meaningful data collection campaign. Paid plans start at $19 per month (billed annually) and provide 2,000 credits per month, which is a more practical allocation for regular use.

How does Browse AI's credits system work?

Browse AI charges credits per robot run rather than per page visit. One credit equals up to 10 rows of extracted data or one screenshot. Credits are consumed each time a robot executes and produces output; if a robot visits a page but finds no new data rows to extract, it still consumes a credit for the run. Credits reset monthly and do not roll over on standard plans. Larger extraction runs on the Professional plan ($69/mo annually) provide 5,000+ credits per month, which covers substantially more volume than the Personal tier.

What integrations does Browse AI support?

Browse AI connects to over 7,000 apps through native Zapier and Make (formerly Integromat) integrations. Direct API access is available on all paid plans, enabling custom workflows without a middleware platform. Common integration targets include Google Sheets, Airtable, Slack, Notion, HubSpot, and various CRM or database tools. Webhook support lets Browse AI push extracted data to any endpoint that accepts POST requests, making it compatible with most modern automation stacks.

What are the best use cases for Browse AI for small businesses?

Browse AI's most practical applications for small businesses include monitoring competitor pricing pages for changes, extracting leads from public business directories (Google Maps, Yelp, industry listing sites), tracking product availability on supplier or marketplace sites, and keeping tabs on job postings or industry news feeds for market intelligence. The no-code robot builder makes these tasks accessible to operators without technical teams — you describe what you want to extract in plain language and Browse AI builds the robot. The 250+ prebuilt robots for popular sources (Amazon, LinkedIn, Indeed, Zillow) reduce setup time further for common data collection tasks.

Final Verdict

Browse AI earns a rating of 4.5/5. For its target audience — non-technical business users who need structured data from the web without maintaining a scraping codebase — it delivers a level of accessibility and feature depth that justifies the Personal plan's $19/mo price point for most use cases. The 250+ prebuilt robots, AI change detection, 5-minute monitoring intervals, and 7,000+ app integrations address the practical data collection needs of marketers, sales teams, and small business operators without requiring any technical background.

The main caveats are around scale and control. Teams whose data needs grow quickly will find the credit model adds up at higher volumes, and users who need to handle complex, JavaScript-heavy sites with sophisticated bot detection may encounter inconsistency. For those users, a developer-oriented platform like Apify offers more capability at a higher complexity cost. But for the majority of business users who want a reliable, low-maintenance way to extract and monitor web data, Browse AI is the most practical entry point in its category — and the free plan removes the barrier to testing that fit before committing.
