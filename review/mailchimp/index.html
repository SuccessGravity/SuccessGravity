Quick Verdict

Mailchimp is the easiest way to send your first email campaign, and its free plan for up to 500 contacts remains one of the most generous entry points in email marketing. For small businesses, solopreneurs, and early-stage startups that need a polished tool fast, it delivers. The tension appears when your list grows past a few thousand contacts, or when you want genuinely sophisticated automation — at that point, the pricing grows steeply and the platform's limitations become hard to ignore.

Small businesses, bloggers, and first-time email marketers who want to start sending today without a complex setup

What Is Mailchimp?

Mailchimp launched in 2001 and spent two decades becoming the default answer whenever someone asked "which email tool should I use?" It pioneered the freemium model in email marketing, made drag-and-drop design standard, and built an ecosystem of integrations that made it plug into virtually any website platform. Today it serves millions of businesses in over 175 countries and has expanded far beyond email into landing pages, social ads, postcards, and a basic website builder.

That long history is both a strength and a liability. Mailchimp has iterated constantly, but so have its competitors — and many of them were built after Mailchimp, with modern automation architectures from the ground up. The result is a platform that remains excellent for straightforward use cases but shows its seams when you push into advanced territory. Understanding exactly where those seams are is what this review is about.

Key Features

Email campaigns and templates

Mailchimp's campaign builder is genuinely pleasant to use. The drag-and-drop editor loads fast, the template library covers common use cases (newsletters, promotions, announcements, event invites), and the preview-and-test flow is one of the best in the industry. When we tested Mailchimp head-to-head with newer tools, the email creation experience felt the most polished and forgiving — errors are caught early, mobile previews are accurate, and even complex multi-column layouts behave predictably.

Automation (Customer Journeys)

Mailchimp rebranded its automation feature as "Customer Journeys" and gave it a visual builder that mimics the style of more powerful tools. You can create branching paths based on tags, purchase events, email engagement, and date triggers. For simple workflows — welcome sequences, birthday emails, post-purchase follow-ups — it works well. Where the automation falls short is depth: compared to platforms like ActiveCampaign, the branching logic is less granular, conditional splits are more limited, and integrating CRM data into journey conditions requires workarounds.

Landing pages

Mailchimp includes a landing page builder on all plans, including the free tier. The pages are clean and responsive, and you can host them on a Mailchimp subdomain or a custom domain. They connect directly to your lists, making list-building straightforward without needing a separate tool like Unbounce or Leadpages for basic use cases.

Audience management and segmentation

Mailchimp uses an "audience" model (what most tools call a list) and supports tags, segments, and groups. The segmentation engine is solid for standard criteria like location, engagement level, purchase history, and survey responses. The most frustrating aspect we found is the audience-based billing model: if the same contact exists in two audiences, you're billed for them twice. Most tools charge once per unique contact — Mailchimp's approach can meaningfully inflate costs for businesses with multiple product lines or brand segments.

Integrations

Mailchimp connects to 300+ apps, including Shopify, WooCommerce, Stripe, Eventbrite, WordPress, and all major social platforms. The integrations are generally reliable and well-documented, which is one of the reasons it remains popular despite stronger competitors on pure feature counts.

Who Should Use Mailchimp?

  • Early-stage businesses that want professional email campaigns running within an afternoon, without a learning curve.
  • Bloggers and content creators building an audience from scratch — the free plan covers the first 500 subscribers with no commitment.
  • Local businesses that need occasional promotional emails and don't require sophisticated segmentation or behavioral triggers.
  • Side projects and small nonprofits where budget is tight and the free tier's limitations are acceptable.

What genuinely surprised us was how many mid-sized businesses are still running on Mailchimp out of habit rather than by deliberate choice — they outgrew it months ago but haven't migrated. If your automation requirements have evolved past simple welcome sequences, it's worth taking a serious look at the alternatives below.

Top Alternatives

  • ActiveCampaign — the most logical next step when you need multi-step automation, lead scoring, and a built-in CRM. More powerful and, past a certain list size, comparably priced.
  • GetResponse — a solid all-rounder with webinar hosting and a more generous automation builder at similar price points.
  • MailerLite — lighter and cheaper than Mailchimp, with a free plan that goes up to 1,000 contacts. A better free-tier comparison if your list is small.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Mailchimp have a free plan?

Yes. Mailchimp's free plan supports up to 500 contacts and lets you send 1,000 emails per month. It includes the drag-and-drop email builder, basic templates, and a single-step automation such as a welcome email. It does not include multi-step automations, A/B testing, or phone support. For a beginner starting from zero, it is one of the most practical free tiers in the category.

Mailchimp vs ActiveCampaign — which is better?

It depends on your stage. Mailchimp wins on ease of entry and brand recognition; ActiveCampaign wins on automation depth, CRM capability, and lead scoring. Most growing businesses find they hit Mailchimp's automation ceiling within 12–18 months, at which point the migration to ActiveCampaign is the natural move. If you know automation will be central to your strategy from day one, starting with ActiveCampaign directly saves the migration effort later.

What happens when I exceed Mailchimp's free plan limits?

Once you exceed 500 contacts or 1,000 monthly sends, Mailchimp pauses your sending ability until you upgrade to a paid plan. There is no grace period — the cutoff is firm. You'll receive email warnings as you approach the limits, which gives you time to prepare, but the upgrade prompt can still catch people off guard if they're not watching their contact count closely.

Does Mailchimp work with Shopify and other e-commerce platforms?

Yes. Mailchimp offers native integrations with Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, and Magento. The Shopify integration syncs your customer and product data, enabling abandoned cart emails, purchase-based segments, and product recommendation blocks inside your campaigns. It is one of the more mature e-commerce integrations in the email marketing space, though Klaviyo has become the preferred choice for stores that need deeper revenue attribution and more granular behavioral triggers.

Final Verdict

Mailchimp earns its rating of 4.4/5 because it genuinely delivers on what it promises: fast setup, excellent email creation tools, and a free tier that lets you validate email as a channel before spending a dollar. The platform's weaknesses — pricing that scales steeply, automation limitations, and the double-billing quirk for multi-audience accounts — matter more the longer you stay on it and the faster you grow. For beginners, it remains a strong starting point. For businesses that have moved past the basics, the alternatives have largely caught up and, in several key areas, pulled ahead.
