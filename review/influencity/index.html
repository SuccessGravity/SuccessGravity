What Is Influencity?

Influencity is a full-cycle influencer marketing platform founded in Barcelona. It positions itself at the intersection of influencer discovery and campaign management — offering a creator search engine, audience analytics, campaign planning tools, relationship tracking, and performance reporting under a single interface. The platform's most prominent claim is a database of over 300 million creator profiles across Instagram, YouTube, and TikTok, enriched with audience demographic data, engagement metrics, and authenticity analysis.

Unlike Heepsy, which focuses almost entirely on the discovery and vetting phase, Influencity extends into campaign execution: brands and agencies can use it to plan activations, track content publishing, measure performance against benchmarks, and generate client-facing reports. This fuller workflow is the reason for its higher price point and why its typical customer skews toward agencies and brands with established influencer programs rather than first-timers dipping a toe in.

Influencity also offers GDPR certification and global data residency options, which matters to European brands and agencies with data compliance requirements. The platform is cloud-based and accessible from any modern browser, with no desktop client required.

Key Features

Creator search with advanced filtering

Influencity's discovery interface lets you search its claimed 300M+ creator index by platform (Instagram, YouTube, TikTok), follower count range, engagement rate, location (country and city), content category, and language. Filters can be stacked to narrow results to very specific niches — for example, fitness creators in Spain with 50,000–200,000 Instagram followers and an engagement rate above 2.5%. The depth of location and audience demographic filtering is one of the features that differentiates Influencity from cheaper tools that offer keyword-based search without the audience-level data layered underneath. According to third-party review aggregators (G2, Capterra), the size and freshness of the database is generally rated positively, though some reviewers note that inactive or low-quality profiles appear in results.

Audience analytics and authenticity

Each creator profile in Influencity includes an audience breakdown: geographic distribution of followers, gender and age range, audience quality indicators (a measure of the proportion of followers that appear to be genuine, active accounts), and an engagement rate relative to platform averages. Influencity's audience analytics go deeper than basic demographics — the platform groups a creator's audience by clusters of similar accounts, which can reveal whether a creator's following is concentrated in a single niche or broad and fragmented across interests. This information helps brands assess fit beyond raw follower count: a creator with 200,000 followers primarily in your target market's core demographic is worth more than one with 500,000 followers spread thinly across unrelated interests.

Campaign forecasting

One of Influencity's differentiating features is campaign forecasting — the ability to model expected reach, impressions, and engagement for a planned campaign before activating it. You add a list of creators to a campaign plan, and the platform estimates the combined reach and predicted engagement based on historical performance data for each creator. This gives media planners and account managers a way to sanity-check campaign proposals against client expectations before committing budgets. The forecasts are estimates based on past performance rather than guaranteed outcomes, but they add a layer of analytical discipline that spreadsheet-based planning cannot replicate.

Multi-campaign and multi-client workspace

Influencity's workspace structure is designed for agencies running multiple concurrent campaigns. Campaigns are organised in separate workspaces, with reporting and creator lists compartmentalised so that content and data from one client's campaign does not bleed into another's. Reviewers on G2 and Capterra highlight this multi-client structure as a key reason agencies prefer Influencity over tools built around a single-brand workflow. User permissions allow team members to be assigned to specific workspaces without seeing other client data.

Relationship management and reporting

Influencity provides a CRM-style creator relationship layer: contact notes, status tracking through the outreach-to-publication workflow, communication logs, and the ability to tag creators by relationship stage. Performance reports can be generated per campaign with customisable metrics and exported as PDFs or data files — useful for agencies presenting results to brand clients without manually compiling screenshots and spreadsheet tables. The reporting module receives positive mentions in reviews as one of the features that saves agencies meaningful hours on client-facing deliverables.

Who Should Use Influencity?

Influencity fits a specific organisational profile best:

  • Influencer marketing agencies managing multiple brand clients simultaneously, where the multi-client workspace, white-label reports, and campaign forecasting pay for themselves in reduced manual overhead.
  • Mid-market in-house teams at brands running 10+ creator partnerships per quarter who need structured campaign management and performance forecasting beyond what a spreadsheet provides.
  • Brands with international programs that need granular audience geography data — particularly European brands with GDPR compliance requirements.
  • Media planners who need to present campaign proposals with reach and engagement projections before approvals.

Influencity is not the right fit for solo marketers running one or two campaigns a year, or small direct-to-consumer brands whose primary measurement need is attributing Instagram posts to Shopify revenue. Those use cases are better served by Heepsy (for affordable discovery) or Grin and Upfluence (for e-commerce attribution).

Alternatives

  • Heepsy — The accessible entry point for influencer discovery, starting ~$49/month. Lacks campaign management depth, but delivers solid creator search and audience authenticity scoring at a fraction of Influencity's cost for brands at earlier stages of their influencer programs.
  • Upfluence — Enterprise platform with strong e-commerce integrations (Shopify, WooCommerce) and an AI outreach co-pilot. Custom pricing similar to Influencity. The better choice for DTC e-commerce brands prioritising revenue attribution over multi-campaign workspace structure.
  • Grin — Another enterprise-tier platform with deep Shopify attribution and creator relationship management, starting approximately $399/month per some third-party sources. Targets established e-commerce brands with dedicated influencer teams.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does Influencity cost?

Third-party pricing sources (PricingSaaS, GetApp) report the Professional plan at approximately $398/month and the Business plan at approximately $998/month. A Basic tier around $168–$198/month has been reported by some aggregators but is unverified. Enterprise pricing requires a custom quote. Annual billing typically saves 15–20% versus monthly. Always confirm current pricing on influencity.com before purchasing.

Does Influencity have a free plan?

No permanently free plan exists. According to Capterra's listing, Influencity offers a 7-day free trial. This is enough time to evaluate the creator search quality and analytics depth before committing.

Is Influencity good for agencies?

Yes — Influencity's multi-campaign workspace, white-label reporting, and permission controls make it well-suited for agencies managing multiple client influencer programs. G2 and Capterra reviewers specifically mention the client reporting features as a time-saving strength.

How does Influencity compare to Upfluence?

Both target agencies and scaling brands, but Upfluence is stronger for e-commerce brands needing Shopify revenue attribution and AI-powered outreach. Influencity is better for agencies prioritising campaign forecasting, audience cluster analytics, and multi-client workspace management. Pricing for both requires a sales conversation.

Final Verdict

Influencity is a capable, agency-grade influencer marketing platform that earns its place in the market through campaign forecasting, deep audience analytics, and a multi-client workspace structure that discovery-only tools cannot match. At approximately $398/month on the entry professional tier (per third-party pricing sources), it represents a meaningful investment — one that makes sense for agencies and mid-market brands running consistent, structured influencer programs, but not for smaller brands running occasional one-off activations.

Its key differentiator versus Heepsy is workflow depth: Influencity takes you from discovery through planning, execution tracking, and client reporting in a single environment. Its key limitation versus Grin and Upfluence is e-commerce attribution: if Shopify revenue per creator is your north-star metric, those platforms are better-tuned for that job. Influencity sits squarely in the middle — a strong choice for the agency and analytics-first use case.
