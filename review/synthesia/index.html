What Is Synthesia?

Synthesia is a browser-based AI video generation platform that lets you turn a written script into a professional-looking video featuring a photorealistic AI avatar — no camera, no studio, no talent fee required. Founded in 2017 and headquartered in London, Synthesia has become the dominant player in enterprise AI video, used by companies like Heineken, Zoom, and the BBC for training, onboarding, compliance, and internal communications.

The premise is straightforward: you type (or paste) your script, choose an avatar from a library of over 140 pre-built presenters, pick a language, and Synthesia renders a video with the avatar speaking your text in lip-synced audio. You can layer in slides, screen recordings, images, background music, and branded templates — all from the browser, in minutes rather than the hours a live-action shoot would take.

What separates Synthesia from earlier text-to-video tools is the quality jump introduced by its Express-2 avatar technology, which adds full-body movement, natural hand gestures, and significantly improved facial expressiveness. Earlier AI avatars were head-and-shoulders crops with minimal motion; Express-2 avatars feel closer to a composed, seated presenter in a real broadcast — still clearly artificial on close inspection, but convincing enough for most professional contexts.

Key Features

Express-2 AI avatars with full-body movement

Synthesia's library includes 140+ pre-built avatars representing a wide range of ages, ethnicities, and presentation styles — casual, business, business-casual — rendered at 1080p. The Express-2 generation, available across the main avatar library, produces fluid hand and arm movement synchronized to speech cadence, so the presenter doesn't look frozen from the shoulders down. Head tilts, weight shifts, and natural blinking complete the impression. For instructional content — where the goal is clarity rather than emotional connection — these avatars clear the believability bar for most enterprise audiences.

On Creator and Enterprise plans, you can also create a personal AI avatar by recording a short consent video (roughly 30 minutes of footage). Synthesia produces a digital twin that speaks any future script in your own likeness, making it possible for a department head to "appear" in 20 different training modules without sitting for a single shoot.

120+ language dubbing and localization

Synthesia's multilingual capability is one of its sharpest competitive advantages. You write your script once, and Synthesia can generate the same video dubbed into more than 120 languages — with the avatar's lips re-synced to the target language's phonetics. For global enterprises running compliance training across offices in Germany, Japan, Brazil, and the United States, this removes what used to be a substantial localization bottleneck. Voicing quality varies by language (high-traffic languages like Spanish, French, and Mandarin are noticeably better than lower-resource languages), but across the tier-one set, it is more than adequate for workplace content.

SCORM export and LMS integration

For learning and development teams, Synthesia's native SCORM export is a decisive feature. Most AI video tools output an MP4 file and leave you to handle LMS packaging yourself. Synthesia exports directly to SCORM 1.2 and SCORM 2004, meaning you can upload the output straight to platforms like Cornerstone, Docebo, SAP SuccessFactors, or Moodle and get completion tracking, pass/fail scoring, and quiz embedding — all without a separate authoring tool. Combined with the platform's built-in quiz overlay, it covers a meaningful slice of what a dedicated course authoring tool like Articulate Storyline would otherwise handle.

Screen recording overlay and real-time collaboration

Synthesia includes a native screen recorder that lets you layer a software walkthrough or interface demonstration alongside an avatar, picture-in-picture style. For IT onboarding, software training, or product tutorials, this removes the need for a separate screen recording tool in many workflows. The platform also supports real-time collaboration — multiple team members can work on a project simultaneously, comment on scenes, and review versions — which matters for L&D teams where instructional designers, subject matter experts, and brand reviewers each have a hand in final sign-off.

Template library and media integration

Synthesia provides a library of 60+ professionally designed video templates covering training, onboarding, product demos, and marketing use cases. Each template handles layout, typography, and animation so that a non-designer can produce on-brand output by swapping in a logo, brand colors, and script. The platform also pulls from a built-in media library of stock images and video clips, and accepts uploads of your own assets — slides, images, audio, and custom background videos — to extend beyond the defaults.

Who Should Use Synthesia?

Synthesia earns its place most decisively in a handful of recurring use cases:

  • Corporate L&D and HR teams that produce regular training, compliance, and onboarding content. Replacing a quarterly video shoot with Synthesia typically pays back the annual subscription in the first module.
  • Internal communications teams at mid-to-large organizations that want a consistent, professional on-screen presenter without relying on executives to sit for video recordings.
  • Global companies running multilingual content programs. The ability to localize into 120+ languages from a single source script is genuinely difficult to replicate with alternatives at comparable cost.
  • SaaS and software companies producing product walkthroughs and release notes videos, where the screen recording overlay keeps things concise and the avatar handles narration without scheduling a recording session.
  • Small businesses and solo operators who want explainer or product demo videos but don't have the budget for a production crew. At $18 per month, the barrier to professional-looking output is lower than it has ever been.

Where Synthesia underperforms: if your brand equity is built on a real person's face and voice, an AI avatar undermines the authenticity that makes that brand work. Agencies producing emotionally resonant consumer advertising, coaches whose following is tied to personal presence, and B2C campaigns that depend on trust-building through human connection should look at tools that enhance real footage rather than replace the person entirely.

Top Alternatives

  • HeyGen — a close competitor with similarly strong avatar quality and a voice-cloning feature that replicates your own voice across all languages. Worth testing side-by-side with Synthesia on the free tiers to compare avatar style and voice preference.
  • ActiveCampaign — not a direct video alternative, but if your goal is distributing training or marketing content to segmented audiences via email automation, pairing Synthesia's video output with ActiveCampaign's delivery infrastructure is a common enterprise workflow.
  • Pictory — better if your primary workflow is repurposing long-form content (webinar recordings, blog posts, podcasts) into short clips. Synthesia creates video from scratch; Pictory edits and clips video that already exists.
  • Loom — better for async communication videos where authenticity matters and production polish does not. Real face, real voice, no edit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Synthesia have a free plan?

Yes. Synthesia offers a free plan that lets you create up to 3 minutes of video per month with a limited selection of avatars and templates. It's enough to evaluate the platform's quality and workflow, but not enough for regular production use. Paid plans start at around $18 per month billed annually.

How realistic are Synthesia AI avatars?

Synthesia's Express-2 avatars are among the most convincing in the market for professional, neutral-toned content. Full-body movement, natural blinking, and precise lip sync make them work well for training videos, product explainers, and internal communications. For highly emotional or persuasive sales content the stiffness can still be noticeable — the avatars rarely convey urgency or warmth convincingly — but for factual, instructional material most workplace audiences accept them without comment.

Can I create a custom avatar in Synthesia?

Yes, on Creator and Enterprise plans you can create a personal AI avatar by recording a short consent video. The process takes roughly 30 minutes of footage, and Synthesia produces a digital twin you can use across unlimited future videos. Custom avatars are ideal for companies that want a consistent on-screen presenter — a real employee's likeness — without scheduling studio time for every new piece of content.

Is Synthesia good for small businesses?

It depends on your use case. If you regularly produce training videos, onboarding materials, or product explainers, the Starter plan at around $18 per month pays for itself quickly versus hiring a production crew or freelance videographer. If your primary need is social media content, short ads, or personal brand video, tools built specifically for those formats may suit you better. The free plan lets you test the fit before committing.

Final Verdict

Synthesia earns a rating of 4.5/5. For the use case it is built for — scalable, professional video production without a production budget — it is the most capable tool in the market right now. The Express-2 avatar quality, 120+ language dubbing, and native SCORM export are genuine differentiators that matter to the enterprise and mid-market buyers who make up the bulk of its audience.

The ceiling shows at the edges: high-emotion content, personal brand video, and social-native formats are not where Synthesia shines. But for any organization spending significant time and money on video production for internal audiences, it is worth a serious look — starting with the free plan to confirm the avatar style and workflow suit your team before committing to a paid tier.
