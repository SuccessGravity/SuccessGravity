What Is Shopify?

Shopify is a fully hosted ecommerce platform that handles everything an online store needs — the storefront, product catalog, checkout, payments, hosting, and security — in one subscription. Launched in 2006, it now powers millions of stores worldwide, from weekend side hustles to billion-dollar brands like Gymshark and Allbirds.

"Hosted" is the key word. Unlike WooCommerce or Magento, there's no server to manage, no security patches to apply, and no 3 a.m. panic when a traffic spike hits during a sale. Shopify's pitch is simple: you run the business, they run the technology. After years of testing store builders, we think no platform delivers on that promise as completely.

Key Features

Store builder & themes

Shopify's theme editor is a drag-and-drop system built around sections and blocks, with a theme store of free and paid designs that are fast, mobile-first, and conversion-tested. You can launch a professional-looking store in an afternoon without writing code, and developers can go deeper with Liquid templating when needed. Shopify Magic, the platform's built-in AI, can draft product descriptions, edit product photos, and answer setup questions along the way.

Checkout & Shopify Payments

This is Shopify's real moat. Its checkout is widely regarded as the highest-converting in the industry, and Shop Pay — the one-tap accelerated checkout used by tens of millions of buyers — routinely lifts conversion further. Shopify Payments handles cards, wallets, and local payment methods natively, with no separate gateway setup. One catch worth knowing upfront: if you use a third-party gateway instead, Shopify adds an extra transaction fee on top of your processor's cut.

App store & multichannel selling

The app store holds 8,000+ apps covering reviews, subscriptions, loyalty, upsells, shipping, taxes — if you can imagine a store feature, someone has built it. Sales channels are equally broad: sell on Instagram, TikTok, Amazon, and other marketplaces from one dashboard, with inventory and orders synced automatically. Built-in abandoned cart recovery emails recapture shoppers who bail at checkout, and the analytics suite shows exactly where revenue comes from.

POS & selling in person

Shopify POS turns a phone or card reader into a register that shares inventory, customers, and gift cards with your online store. For brands doing markets, pop-ups, or full retail locations, having one system of record for online and offline sales is a genuine operational win most competitors can't match.

Global selling with Markets

Shopify Markets lets one store sell internationally with localized currencies, languages, duties estimates, and local domains — features that used to require separate storefronts or expensive apps. For stores with cross-border ambitions, it's quietly one of the platform's best features.

Pros and Cons

✓ Pros

  • Industry-leading checkout conversion and Shop Pay
  • Fast, easy store setup — no servers or code required
  • 8,000+ apps and true multichannel selling
  • Unified POS for online + in-person retail
  • Scales from side hustle to enterprise (Plus)

✗ Cons

  • Extra transaction fees without Shopify Payments
  • App subscriptions stack up fast ($50–300+/mo is common)
  • Theme and checkout customization limited below Plus
  • Blogging and content tools are weak next to WordPress

Who Should Use Shopify?

  • New store owners who want the shortest path from idea to first sale, with hosting, security, and payments handled for them.
  • Growing DTC brands that need reliable infrastructure, high-converting checkout, and room to scale without replatforming.
  • Retailers with physical locations that want online and in-store inventory, customers, and sales in one system via POS.
  • Multichannel sellers pushing products to Instagram, TikTok, and Amazon who need a single source of truth.

If your site is 90% content and 10% commerce — a blog that sells a course or a few products — Shopify is the wrong center of gravity. Build on WordPress and add commerce, not the other way around.

Top Alternatives

  • WooCommerce — better if you want full code-level control on WordPress and don't mind managing hosting, updates, and security yourself. The strongest choice for content-heavy stores.
  • Wix — better for very small, simple stores where the website matters as much as the shop and budgets are tight.
  • BigCommerce — better for B2B sellers who need native price lists, customer groups, and quote workflows without an app stack.

Whichever platform you pick, strong product visuals move the needle most — we pair every store build with Canva for banners, product mockups, and social creatives.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Shopify have a free plan?

No. Shopify offers a 3-day free trial, usually followed by a promotional first-month deal (often $1/month). After that, the Basic plan runs about $29/month billed annually. There is no permanently free tier.

What are Shopify's transaction fees?

If you use Shopify Payments, you pay only standard card processing rates — no extra platform fee. If you use a third-party gateway like PayPal or Stripe directly, Shopify adds an additional fee of roughly 0.5–2% per sale depending on your plan. For most stores, using Shopify Payments is the obvious move.

Shopify vs WooCommerce — which is better?

Shopify wins on ease, reliability, and checkout conversion; WooCommerce wins on flexibility, content, and avoiding platform fees. If you'd rather run a business than a website, choose Shopify. If you want total control and already live in WordPress, WooCommerce makes sense.

Is the Starter plan enough to run a store?

Only if you sell exclusively through social media, DMs, or shareable links — Starter (~$5/month) doesn't include a full online storefront. The moment you want a real website with your own branding and navigation, you'll need Basic or above.

Final Verdict

Shopify earns its rating of 4.8/5. No platform makes launching, running, and scaling an online store this frictionless, and the checkout alone — the highest-converting we've tested — can pay for the subscription. Watch the app-stack creep and use Shopify Payments to dodge extra fees, and it's simply the safest bet in ecommerce for 2026.
