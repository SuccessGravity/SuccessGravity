What Is DistroKid?

DistroKid is a music distribution platform that sends your recordings to major streaming services and digital stores — Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Music, TikTok, YouTube Music, and more than 150 other platforms. Founded in 2013 by Philip Kaplan, it has grown into one of the largest independent distributors by catalog size, with millions of artists uploading music through the platform.

The model is straightforward: pay an annual subscription fee and you can upload as many songs and albums as you want. DistroKid delivers them to streaming platforms and passes through 100% of the royalties it collects on your behalf. There are no per-release fees and no percentage cuts — the subscription is the only cost.

Key Features

Unlimited music distribution

Unlike some distributors that charge per single or album, every DistroKid plan allows unlimited uploads. For artists who release frequently — multiple singles a month, album projects, remixes, side projects under different names — this structure delivers enormous value. The annual cost is effectively a few dollars per release for high-volume creators.

100% royalty retention

DistroKid does not take a percentage of your streaming or download royalties. The platform earns its revenue from the annual subscription fee alone. According to DistroKid's help documentation, 100% of net royalties collected from DSPs (digital service providers) are paid to your DistroKid account for withdrawal. By contrast, CD Baby retains 9% of download and streaming income for the life of a release.

Distribution network

DistroKid distributes to Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Music Unlimited, TikTok, YouTube Music, Pandora, iHeart, Deezer, Tidal, and more than 150 other platforms. Artists can opt in or out of specific stores during the upload process.

Spotify for Artists verification

When you distribute through DistroKid, you can apply for Spotify for Artists verification, which unlocks blue-check badges, playlist pitching tools, and canvas video uploads. This is standard across major distributors, but it matters for artists building a Spotify presence.

Store customization

DistroKid lets you choose which stores receive each release. You can include or exclude specific platforms, set custom prices in download stores, and configure YouTube Content ID registration (which claims ad revenue when others use your music in videos).

Who Should Use DistroKid?

  • Prolific indie artists releasing multiple singles or projects per year — the unlimited upload model makes DistroKid dramatically cheaper per release than per-release fee services.
  • Bedroom producers and creators who want TikTok and YouTube Music distribution for original sounds without major-label overhead.
  • Independent labels managing multiple artists — the Ultimate plan at ~$89.99/year supports up to 100 artist names.
  • Podcasters and spoken-word creators distributing audio to music platforms alongside Spotify podcast hosting.

If you release music rarely — one or two projects every few years — a one-time-fee distributor like CD Baby may be more economical in the long run, since you won't need to maintain an annual subscription to keep catalog live.

Alternatives to DistroKid

  • CD Baby — charges a one-time fee per release ($9.99/single, $14.99/album) and takes 9% of royalties. Better if you release rarely and want music live permanently without annual renewals.
  • TuneCore — similar annual subscription model; slightly higher pricing but includes some music publishing administration features on higher tiers.
  • Amuse — free tier available with paid upgrades; good for artists testing the waters before committing to a paid distributor.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does DistroKid take a percentage of royalties?

No. DistroKid keeps 0% of your streaming and download royalties — you keep 100%. The only revenue it earns is your annual subscription fee. This is a key differentiator versus CD Baby, which takes a 9% cut of download and streaming revenue for the life of a release.

What happens to my music if I cancel DistroKid?

If your subscription lapses, DistroKid will begin removing your releases from streaming platforms. This is different from one-time-fee distributors like CD Baby, where your music stays live permanently. If staying live without renewing is important to you, DistroKid offers a "Leave a Legacy" feature that keeps selected releases live for a one-time fee.

How fast does DistroKid deliver music to Spotify?

According to DistroKid's help center, delivery to most platforms takes three to five business days on average, though Spotify can sometimes process new releases in as little as one to two days. Pre-save campaigns and scheduled release dates require the Musician Plus plan or higher.

Is DistroKid good for small artists?

Yes — the Musician plan at $24.99 per year is one of the most affordable ways for a solo artist to distribute unlimited music to 150+ platforms while keeping 100% of royalties. The main limitation is that daily streaming stats and custom release dates require upgrading to Musician Plus ($44.99/yr).
