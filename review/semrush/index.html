What Is Semrush?

Semrush is an all-in-one SEO and digital marketing platform that covers the entire visibility workflow: finding keywords, spying on competitors, auditing your site's technical health, building links, tracking rankings, and optimizing content. Launched in 2008 as a competitor research tool, it has grown into a suite of 55+ tools used by millions of marketers, from freelancers to Fortune 500 teams.

What sets Semrush apart is breadth backed by data. Its keyword database spans more than 25 billion keywords across 140+ geographic databases, and its competitive intelligence covers both organic and paid search. Instead of stitching together a keyword tool, a rank tracker, an audit crawler, and a backlink checker, you get all of them reading from the same datasets — which is exactly why so many agencies standardize on it.

Key Features

Keyword research (Keyword Magic Tool)

The Keyword Magic Tool is the best starting point we've used for keyword research. Type a seed term and it returns thousands of related keywords grouped by topic, with volume, keyword difficulty, search intent, and SERP features for each. Filtering by question keywords or low-difficulty terms makes it easy to find realistic targets, and with 25B+ keywords in the database, even niche industries surface plenty of ideas.

Competitor analysis

This is Semrush's original superpower. Drop in any domain and you can see the keywords it ranks for, its estimated traffic, its top pages, and — crucially — the keyword gaps between you and up to four competitors. The same lens works for paid search: you can see a rival's Google Ads keywords and ad copy history, which is intelligence most tools simply don't offer.

Site Audit

The Site Audit crawler runs 140+ technical checks — broken links, crawlability issues, Core Web Vitals, duplicate content, hreflang errors, and more — then prioritizes them by severity with plain-English explanations of why each matters. Scheduled recrawls track your health score over time, so you can prove that fixes actually moved the needle.

Backlink analytics & link building

Semrush's backlink index has improved dramatically over the years and now stands close to the category leaders. You get referring-domain analysis, toxic-link auditing, and a Link Building Tool that turns competitor backlink gaps into an outreach pipeline with built-in email tracking — a workflow that usually requires a separate outreach app.

Content marketing toolkit

On Guru plans and above, the content toolkit adds Topic Research for ideation and the SEO Writing Assistant, which scores drafts for readability, tone, originality, and keyword usage in real time (with Google Docs and WordPress plugins). It won't replace a dedicated optimizer for heavy content teams, but it's a genuinely useful layer on top of the research tools. Rank tracking, PPC research, and a set of social media scheduling tools round out the suite.

Who Should Use Semrush?

  • Agencies and consultants managing multiple client sites who need audits, rank tracking, and white-label-ready reporting under one roof.
  • In-house SEO and content teams that plan strategy around competitor gaps and need to defend budget with hard data.
  • Businesses running both SEO and Google Ads — Semrush is one of the few tools that covers organic and paid research equally well.
  • Serious affiliate and niche site builders whose revenue justifies a professional-grade subscription.

If you publish a few posts a month on a hobby blog, ~$140/mo is hard to justify. Start with the free account, and upgrade only when the data starts paying for itself.

Top Alternatives

  • Ahrefs — better if backlink analysis is your top priority; its backlink index strength is still the benchmark, though its all-around toolkit is narrower.
  • Surfer SEO — better if on-page content optimization is your main job; it goes deeper on content scoring than Semrush's Writing Assistant, at a fraction of the price.
  • Moz — better for beginners who want a friendlier interface and a gentler learning curve, trading away some data depth.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Semrush have a free plan?

Yes — a free account lets you run a limited number of searches per day, track a small set of keywords, and audit a small site. It's enough to learn the interface, but any real workload will hit the daily limits quickly. Pro and Guru plans also come with a 7-day free trial.

Is Semrush worth it for a small blog?

Only if the blog earns (or will soon earn) enough to cover ~$139.95/mo. For hobby sites, use the free account or a cheaper focused tool, and upgrade when your traffic revenue justifies professional data.

Semrush vs Ahrefs — which is better?

They're the two heavyweights. Ahrefs still edges ahead on backlink index strength and simplicity; Semrush wins on breadth — PPC research, content tools, site audits, and social features Ahrefs doesn't match. If you only do link-focused SEO, pick Ahrefs; for everything else, Semrush is the more complete platform.

Can multiple people use one Semrush account?

Each license includes a single user seat, and sharing logins violates the terms (concurrent sessions get flagged). Additional seats can be purchased on any plan, which is a real cost factor for teams — budget for it when comparing plans.

Final Verdict

Semrush earns its rating of 4.7/5. No other platform covers as much of the search marketing workflow this well: the keyword and competitor data are elite, the site audit is genuinely actionable, and the paid-search intelligence is something rivals barely attempt. The price stings for solo creators and the single-seat policy is an annoyance for teams — but if search traffic drives your business, Semrush remains the toolkit we'd buy first in 2026.
