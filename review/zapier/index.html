What Is Zapier?

Zapier is a no-code automation platform that connects the apps you use every day — Gmail, Slack, Salesforce, Shopify, Google Sheets, Notion, and thousands more — so that actions in one app automatically trigger actions in another. Founded in 2011, Zapier has grown into the world's largest automation marketplace, with over 7,000 app integrations covering virtually every software category: CRMs, email marketing tools, project management platforms, ecommerce solutions, payment processors, and social media networks.

The core unit of automation in Zapier is a Zap: a rule that says "when this happens in App A, do this in App B." A simple example: when a new row is added to a Google Sheet, create a task in Asana and send a Slack notification. Multi-step Zaps let you chain as many actions together as you need — and Paths let you add conditional branching, so different outcomes happen depending on the data. The whole thing is built through a step-by-step editor that requires zero coding knowledge.

The platform is built to minimize the friction between deciding to automate something and actually having it live, with a guided editor and a large library of ready-made templates for common workflows.

Key Features

Multi-step Zaps

While single-step Zaps (one trigger, one action) are available on the free plan, multi-step Zaps are where Zapier earns its reputation. A single Zap can chain together dozens of sequential actions across multiple apps — filter the data, format it, look up a record, create or update an entry elsewhere, and send a notification, all in one automated flow. A lead enrichment workflow is a typical example: pull form submissions through a data enrichment API, score the lead, create a CRM contact, assign a salesperson, and fire a Slack message — all from a single trigger.

Paths (conditional logic)

Paths allow a single Zap to branch into different outcomes based on conditions you set. If a deal value is above $5,000, route it one way; if it's below, route it another. You can create up to four separate paths in a single Zap on the Professional plan, each with its own set of actions. Compared to Make's more visual flow-based approach, Zapier's Paths feel slightly more like filling out a form than drawing a flowchart — which is either a feature or a limitation depending on how your team thinks. For most non-technical users, the form-style UI is faster to get right the first time.

AI-powered Zaps

Zapier has integrated AI throughout its platform in ways that are genuinely useful rather than just cosmetic. The most practical is AI by Zapier — a native step that can summarize text, classify content, extract structured data from unstructured inputs, or generate a draft response, all without a separate ChatGPT or Claude integration. There's also an AI-powered Zap builder that lets you describe what you want in plain English and generates a starting Zap for you to refine. As with any AI-generated draft, check the field mappings before activating. As a drafting tool that saves you from building from scratch, it's a meaningful time-saver.

Tables & Interfaces

Relatively newer additions to the Zapier platform, Tables and Interfaces extend Zapier from pure automation into lightweight internal tooling. Tables is a native database that your Zaps can read from and write to — useful for storing data that doesn't belong in any specific app but needs to be shared across multiple workflows. Interfaces is a low-code tool for building simple internal forms and pages that can trigger Zaps directly. Together they let you build basic internal apps — a lead intake form that scores and routes submissions, for example — entirely within Zapier, without exporting the work to a separate tool like Airtable or Notion.

Integrations ecosystem (7,000+ apps)

The sheer breadth of Zapier's integration library is its most defensible advantage. With over 7,000 apps — roughly three to four times the size of Make's library and an order of magnitude larger than most competitors — Zapier connects software that no other platform supports. Niche project management tools, regional payment processors, specialized industry software: if it has an API, there's a good chance someone has already built a Zapier integration for it. For businesses running a diverse tool stack that includes a few less-common apps, this alone makes Zapier the default choice.

Who Should Use Zapier?

Zapier earns a place in the stack for a specific set of use cases:

  • Solopreneurs and small businesses that run across many different SaaS tools and want them to talk to each other without hiring a developer. The free plan covers basic needs; Starter handles most solo workflows.
  • Marketing teams automating lead handoff: form submission to CRM, CRM to email sequence, email click to sales notification — all without custom code or engineering involvement.
  • Operations teams connecting HR tools, communication platforms, and project management software to reduce manual data entry and keep records in sync.
  • Agencies and consultants that need to connect a client's unusual app stack quickly. Zapier's breadth makes it the most likely tool to support whatever combination of apps a client has chosen.

Where Zapier is a harder fit: high-volume data pipelines where per-task billing becomes prohibitive, or workflows requiring complex data transformation logic where Make's more code-friendly approach is more efficient.

Top Alternatives

  • Make (formerly Integromat) — the closest competitor, with a visual canvas that handles complex logic better and significantly lower per-operation pricing. Ideal for technical users and agencies running high-volume workflows. See our Zapier vs Make comparison.
  • n8n — an open-source alternative with self-hosting options and a generous free cloud tier. Better for developers who want full control; steeper learning curve for non-technical teams.
  • ActiveCampaign — if the bulk of your automation need is around email marketing and CRM behavior triggers, a dedicated platform often handles that specific domain better than a general-purpose connector like Zapier.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Zapier have a free plan?

Yes. Zapier offers a permanently free plan that includes up to 100 tasks per month and single-step Zaps connecting two apps. It's a useful starting point for light automation — a form-to-spreadsheet sync, for example — but most practical workflows quickly hit the task ceiling. Paid plans start at around $19.99 per month (Starter, billed monthly) and unlock multi-step Zaps, Filters, and a higher task limit.

Zapier vs Make — which is better for automations?

Zapier is the better choice if you want the broadest integration library and the fastest time-to-automation without technical knowledge. Make is the better choice if you need complex conditional logic, lower per-operation pricing at scale, or prefer a visual canvas-style workflow builder. Most non-technical users start with Zapier and find it covers 90% of their needs; technical users and agencies running high-volume workflows often find Make's pricing and logic depth more compelling.

How many tasks does Zapier give you per month?

The free plan includes 100 tasks per month. Starter includes 750, Professional includes 2,000, and Team includes 50,000. Each action a Zap performs on a single piece of data counts as one task — so a 5-step Zap triggered 100 times consumes 500 tasks. You can purchase task top-ups if you exceed your monthly limit, or upgrade to a higher plan. Task counts and plan pricing are subject to change, so always verify the current allocation on Zapier's pricing page.

Is Zapier good for non-technical users?

Yes — Zapier is arguably the most beginner-friendly automation platform available. Its step-by-step Zap editor walks you through each connection, field mapping is mostly handled automatically, and Zapier's library of 6,000+ pre-built Zap templates means you often don't need to build from scratch. Most users are up and running with their first working Zap within 15 minutes of signing up, with no coding or API knowledge required.

Final Verdict

Zapier earns its rating of 4.7/5. No automation platform comes close on sheer integration breadth — 7,000+ apps is a moat that matters the moment your stack includes anything unusual. The platform's maturity shows in its reliability, its documentation, and the quality of most integrations.

The credit-based billing model is the most legitimate frustration for growing teams. For a modest mid-market automation stack — say, 15 active multi-step Zaps running at reasonable volumes — the Professional plan's 2,000 tasks per month can be tight, and the jump to Team at $69/month is steep for a small team. If your workflow demands are moderate and your app stack is standard, Zapier is excellent value. If you're pushing volume or complexity, run the math on Make before committing long-term.

For non-technical users automating their first workflows, or businesses that need broad coverage with minimal setup overhead, Zapier remains the easiest recommendation in its category.
